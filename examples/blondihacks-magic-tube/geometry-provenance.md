# Magic Tube nominal geometry provenance

## Identity and design boundary

- Source video: Blondihacks, *Metal Lathe Tutorial 18: Your Second Project!*,
  [YouTube -dyO4PEkEkc](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-dyO4PEkEkc), uploaded
  2019-07-27. Actual full local MP4 and English automatic captions were acquired;
  no frame dimensions are interpreted as physical measurements.
- Older author article: [*Lathe – Magic Tube*](https://blondihacks.com/lathe-magic-tube/),
  dated 2017-02-04. This is a **different size/process iteration**, not a released
  drawing of the 2019 video. Its 3/8-24 thread and 0.500-in OD are not used.
- Catalog size source: [Continental Steel cold-finished 12L14 round bar,
  12L14-6875](https://continentalsteel.com/item/steel-bars/cold-finished-round-bar-type-12l14/12l14-6875),
  listed in its [12L14 family table](https://continentalsteel.com/viewitems/steel-bars/cold-finished-round-bar-type-12l14)
  as 11/16 in, 0.6875 in, 17.4625 mm. This is round-bar nominal size, **not** a
  catalog specification for a finished screw-cap tube. The final-OD selection
  is an expressly authored adaptation of that real nominal.
- Local design identifier `BH-MT-ADAPTED-EXAMPLE / source-size-design-1` is
  authored for this demonstration. It is not an original drawing/revision,
  manufacturer release or evidence that anyone approved physical production.

The source/choice table in [README.md](README.md#design-basis-without-invented-measurements)
is authoritative for nominal provenance. Most importantly, the older article
publishes **3.5-in overall**, not a 3.5-in body. This design deliberately
reassigns that size to **body length**, retains its 3-in nominal cavity depth
and 0.500-in **head** length, and therefore becomes **4-in closed overall**
with a **0.500-in closed base**. This is a documented author design decision,
not a source measurement or inferred video dimension. The video supplies
1/2-20 RH thread identity, nominal 0.500-in male stub length, 0.700-in
preliminary OD, 1/4-in pilot and 15/32-in tap-drill identities.

Material 12L14 is adopted from the older article/catalog as a design choice.
The 2019 video calls its bar scrap and does not establish its alloy. Actual
material identity, tool sizes beyond the explicitly narrated identities,
stock dimensions, tolerance bands, chamfers and finish limits stay unknown.

## Reproducible native solid construction

`build_part.py` uses FreeCAD's native Part/OpenCascade kernel only:

1. Body: cylinder Ø17.4625 × 88.9 mm, model axis +Z, base Z0.
2. Blind cavity: Ø11.90625 mm, Z12.7 to Z88.9 before the socket-envelope step.
3. Socket visualization: Ø12.7 mm, Z76.2 to Z88.9. This represents one nominal
   male-stub length at **thread major diameter**, not a tapped minor diameter,
   full-thread depth, manufacturer fit or actual engagement.
4. Cap head: cylinder Ø17.4625 mm, Z88.9 to Z101.6.
5. Male visualization: cylinder Ø12.7 mm, Z76.2 to Z88.9, fused only to its
   own head. It is **not** fused to the body.
6. Export a compound containing the two valid separate solids in nominal closed
   position. The cylinders/annuli may coincide at their nominal mating surfaces,
   but no positive-volume overlap or fictitious bonding is introduced.
7. Reimport and require exactly two valid solids; emit `MAGIC_TUBE_JSON` with
   validity, count, volume, per-face geometry and SHA-256.

The generator normalizes only the non-geometric STEP `FILE_NAME` header and LF
line endings. It preserves existing STEP bytes and raises rather than silently
replacing a frozen file whose regenerated bytes differ. FreeCAD's launcher can
return 0 even after a Python exception, so **presence and content of the JSON
success tag matter**, not an exit code alone.

The native `TechDraw.projectToSVG` projection is hidden-line removal on the
actual B-reps. Its output for a compound is multiple SVG group fragments, so
the generator wraps them in one group before parsing. `part-reference.svg`
explodes only the **display copy** of the cap by 15 mm to reveal the two pieces;
that display gap is not a machining dimension or assembly position. The frozen
STEP remains in closed nominal position. No stock or holding geometry is
misrepresented by this illustration.

| Cap volume | 4650.426648982699 mm³ |
| Total nominal volume | 17263.13784426415 mm³ |
| Native body/cap common volume | 0.0 mm³; no positive-volume interference in the nominal visualization |
| STEP SHA-256 | `3c9d4fe47078a61ceda7c5b4e84f6cd5367e6303ba5d05cf3a75aa17a3dca90a` |

These are computed nominal-model facts, not physical measurements or an

physical drill-point profile. Helical threads, leads/runouts, actual minor
and blank diameters, root fillet, mouth chamfer, exposed bevels/edge breaks and
the transient live-centre seat are not modeled. No missing dimension is filled
with a fabricated measurement or tolerance.

## Exact STEP face ownership and native binding

References name the exact adjacent STEP entity, its file-order ADVANCED_FACE
ordinal and its actual empty STEP label. They do **not** mean an imported face
number. A read-only external FreeCAD probe used the repository's `StepFile`
parser to isolate each source entity, loaded the resulting one-face open shell,
and recorded its native type, area, bounds and orientation. The source graph
binds body faces to `CLOSED_SHELL #16 / MANIFOLD_SOLID_BREP #15`, and cap faces
to `CLOSED_SHELL #159 / MANIFOLD_SOLID_BREP #158`. No nearest-face or import-order
fallback is used.

The parser's actual `StepFile.solid_refs()` output is
`#15/MANIFOLD_SOLID_BREP[1]/` and `#158/MANIFOLD_SOLID_BREP[2]/`.
`features.toml` binds those exact source references as `solids.body` and
`solids.cap`. These ordinals are source STEP entity order, never imported
shape/solid order. The labels are genuinely empty in the source STEP.

The mouth/shoulder annuli have equal area 112.82158714280229 mm² and identical
bounds but opposite orientation. The male/socket cylinders have equal area
506.7074790974979 mm² and identical bounds but opposite orientation. The small
socket step annulus `#104` and male-tip disk `#247` have identical bounding
boxes but **different areas** (15.3397771992406 versus 126.67686977437442 mm²),
so they are not exact signature duplicates.

A focused native probe of the original geometry-only matcher observed two
candidate imported faces for each true duplicate pair. This is real ambiguity
in a two-solid closed assembly, not permission to choose by file/import order.
Face ownership/orientation and the native geometry status must be respected;
any unresolved kernel/stock/holding facts remain debt, never a false pass.

## Frame conventions versus physical zero

The model's body base is Z0; mouth and cap shoulder seat are Z88.9; head end
Z101.6; nominal male tip Z76.2. `plan.toml` declares two right-handed frames as
explicit **AUTHOR'S CHOICE**:

- `mouth-out`: origin at body mouth, +Z toward the tailstock; base is setup
  Z-88.9, final cap face Z12.7.
- `cap-stub-out`: origin at male tip; +Z toward the tailstock in the cap-making
  hold, opposite model +Z; shoulder Z-12.7, final head face Z-25.4.

These are exact nominal design conventions only. The source uses mechanical
handwheel/quill scales and dial-indicator travel, not an asserted DRO. Actual
reholding placement, radial/axial references, indicator calibration, tool-tip
compensation, parting-blade corner and stock envelopes remain unknown. A
nominal CAD frame does not close the physical pickup or material-lineage debt.
