# Prechips v0.11

Turn an authored manual-machining process into a printable HTML traveler. Choose a compact professional sheet or a hobby sheet with setup diagrams.

This prototype binds traveler dimensions to a selected CAD/drawing export. Process choices remain authored. The previous implementation is preserved on [`archive/pre-v0.10-2026-11-08`](https://github.com/pedropaulovc/prechips/tree/archive/pre-v0.10-2026-11-08); old code returns only when a useful milestone needs it.

Plans, CAD manifests and shop files are expected to be written by an AI agent, so they are expected to be comprehensive. Built-in profiles require every applicable setup and operation to declare the structured data each check reads, or an explicit opt-out value; nothing is skipped because a field was left out. Values in the inputs are used as written.

The base-bar, end-bracket, feed-lever, riser, carrier, pivot-block, feed-block, leadscrew-kit and arc-strap examples declare their milling beams and cuts and pass `milling_deflection` enforced (see [Milling deflection](#milling-deflection)).

Open the HTML in a browser. Print on Letter paper at 100% scale with browser headers and footers off. Files are standalone; hobby embeds its diagrams, so they need no server, internet connection or adjacent image files.
The output must be a different file from the input plan and every active input, including filesystem aliases and active turning configuration files.

The pro base-bar example stays within two Letter pages at 10.5pt. Hobby includes diagrams of all five setups. Dimensions come from the v50 CAD/drawing export, re-exported when the rail stations became banded drill targets; v50 is the drawing's unreleased working revision, so both exports carry it. The B1–B4 station bands sit on the printed millimetres (47.48 ±0.13 reads 1.8642–1.8744 in). Setup 3 faces both sides, squares both ends and mills both slots in one vise clamping. Every speed is on the PM-30MV's low belt (owner ruling 2026-11-16; the shop's `machines.pm-30mv` record), so no setup needs a belt change. Tool selection, allowances, feeds and speeds remain process choices. Since v48 only bar 1 carries the four #12 counterbored bracket holes. The rail holes are drilled at their printed stations, 9.964 + 60 n mm ±0.05 mm from ZERO END (owner ruling 2026-11-16: no transfer from the rail); each bar's untrimmed HGR15 rail is then fitted and trimmed flush with both ends (assembly steps 1D–1F). The adapter binds `cad.rail_station_tol_mm`, `cad.rail_first_band_mm` and `cad.rail_last_band_mm` from the manifest's station bands and refuses unequal bands; `cad.rail_station_tol_in` is that band for the inch DRO, rounded down to 0.0001 in so it never loosens. Setup 4's vise hold centres the finished bar: the adapter binds `cad.vise_centred_overhang_mm` = (length − `process.vise_jaw_width_mm`)/2. The jack centres stay authored; the adapter refuses one off the bar or inside a rail-hole, counterbore or slot outline. Every milling-deflection station is bound from the adapter, not copied by hand: the jacks and straps, both vise holds' jaw sides (the centred blank in Setup 3, the centred finished bar for the slots and Setup 4), the rail stations by end, the bracket columns, the slot centres and each slot's entry corner nearest its jack. Setup 4's drill and counterbore operations split by end, each over its jack, because the jacks only carry drill thrust.

Drawing sheet 2 swaps bar 2's seats: its rail goes on the face that is up in Setup 4 (marked RAIL SIDE) and the opposite face seats on the mill table, while bar 1's rail goes opposite its C'BORE SIDE, so bar 1 is turned over from Setup 4 to sit C'BORE SIDE down. The v50 manifest describes bar 1 only, so that per-bar face text is authored from the two sheets, not bound. The 1/2 in end mill alone cuts both hold-down slots: with e = (slot width − cutter Ø)/2, the cutter centre traces a rectangle e either side of the arc-centre line and e past each arc centre, which makes the drawn width and overall length. Each end is then two cutter-radius corners, so the end radius departs outward from the drawn arc by e(√2 − 1) (about .006 in). The adapter computes the corners from the manifest and `half_cutter_dia_mm`, prints that cutter size in the operation, and refuses a cutter that is not under the slot width or whose end-radius departure exceeds the drawing's two-place linear tolerance (±.01) applied to the end radius. It also binds `slot_cutter_band_mm`, the slot width band less 2e; the slot operation's `tool_sizing` band checks the shop end mill's `diameter_mm` against it, so a cutter other than the one the offsets assume refuses. Position inspections are bound from the manifest bands: X on the mill DRO before unclamping, rows from ZERO EDGE with calipers.

The end-bracket example prints two pro pages or five hobby pages, one per setup. It follows the 2026-11-02 hand traveler's route and tools, but the v49 drawing wins: two shared-axis #8-32 holes at the KP08 stations, drilled #29 through (tip 1.06 below BAR SIDE, so the KP08-side exit is full size) and tapped plug-then-bottoming from both faces. The export states the thread zones and tip marks only in note text, so the adapter reads them with exact patterns and refuses reworded notes or an inch/mm disagreement. This is the single deliberate prose-parsing exception, kept until the CAD export provides structured `thread_zone`/`tip_mark` fields. Each face's taps carry one authored flag (`bar_tap_flag_in`, `kp08_tap_flag_in`), measured from the tap's physical tip as the drawing's tip marks are, in the low half of its tip window. The adapter refuses a flag that, ± the authored `tap_flag_tol_in`, leaves its window, and a pair whose deepest tips together reach the minimum width, so the two bottoming taps never meet. The end mill runs at the shop's `jig-em-half-6061-t6511` (owner ruling 2026-11-16: the 2026-11-05 logbook's 1800 rpm, 7 in/min, on the low belt); the final inspection calipers the as-bought section. The sheet names the KP08 face FRONT FACE and the base-bar face BACK FACE; the plan marks them KP08 SIDE and BAR SIDE and says so, and its final screw check runs a flagged screw only to its own face's full thread, short of the opposite face's out-of-phase thread. Unlike the hand sheet, both brackets share each vise clamping, one at each jaw end (each balances the other): every operation runs on both before the next tool, X0 sits on the left part's INNER END and XR on the right part's; the KP08-side tapping lines up on the drilled holes. Hobby pictures both blanks from the bar end the lever leaves, both two-up end-squaring holds, each tapping face as a vise top view of both parts with a section through H1 (thread zone and tap flag of the face being tapped), and the finished bracket.

The feed-lever example prints two pro pages or four hobby pages from the 2026-11-02 hand traveler's route and the manifest exported after the owner's 2026-11-13 ruling: the 2X bolt holes are the 11/32 drill, Ø.3438 +.004/0 (the hand sheet's drill; v48/v49 printed the 5/16 normal-fit clearance Ø.344, whose minimum the drill was under), inspected with pin gauges. The manifest's `grip_corners` fillet gives the 2X R.2 grip-end corners and their one-place band; the adapter refuses a manifest without it. Both ends are milled, as the manifest's process note says ("saw and face to length"): ZERO END in the hole setup, then the grip end to length in a second vise hold, its length checked as the hand sheet does, with the edge finder on the grip end read on the DRO from ZERO END (owner ruling 2026-11-16). The adapter binds the end cuts' tip depth below the underside, `cad.end_cut_below_in` from the authored `end_cut_tip_z_mm`, for all four end-cut instructions, and the same Z from the as-bought top, `cad.end_cut_depth_in` (the blank thickness plus that depth), which the hobby texts give as the depth to set from the top face. It refuses a tip that does not reach below the underside, and a depth from the top that, rounded to the two places the hobby text prints, does not pass the thickness band's top (.509): the bar is used as supplied, so a thick bar must still be cut through. The final inspection calipers the as-bought section and runs two 5/16-18 screws through both holes at once. Hobby pictures the lever blank from the bar's squared end, both vise holds and the grip end upright at the end of the bench-vise jaws with its scribed lines and dashed inspection band; the corner operation stops the 45° flat short of the lines and rolls it into the arc.

The riser example prints three pro pages or six hobby pages and passes every check in both profiles, `milling_deflection` included. Its route follows the v49 manifest's process note: a 14.00 in (13.95–14.05) blank of 1/4 × 1-3/4 in 6061 bar, calipered first (any point under .230 rejects the bar) and faced to .240 on the bare table only when it is over .250 (Stage A from three straps, Stage B from four on faced metal; Z0 the table), both long edges milled on edge on one 1 in parallel, the ends and the two #30 stop-pin holes P1/P2 cut flat in the vise with each end .7–.8 past the jaws. In Setup 2 each Stage A span and Stage B's middle strip bend between the nearest strap on each side, no more than 6.8 in apart on the longest (14.05) blank, and Stage B's two end cuts overhang their outer strap, 1.5 in from each end; the adapter derives these supports and passes from the authored strap layout, prints the nominal blank's layout, refuses one the shortest or longest blank cannot take, and gives the beams the longest blank's stations. Both stages' beams take the .230 band bottom: a beam's one section serves its rough and finish passes, and faced metal is the thinnest either meets. Setup 2's envelope takes the declared 1/4 in stock as its work top; no source bounds how far over .250 an incoming bar may be. Because any bar from .230 to .250 skips Setup 2, the Setup 3–5 beams take the .230 minimum thickness, the Setup 3–4 envelopes take the longest blank, and the Setup 4–5 work tops take the .250 band top. The other seven holes (R1–R4, HUB, CL, CR) print X/Y LOC REF and are spotted from the carrier at assembly step 11C, so Setup 5 drills each at its own mark in one of three vise holds and countersinks R1–R4 from the other face; its table prints the REF X/Y only to find the marks. The adapter refuses a toleranced station on a spotted hole, a spotted hole that no hold puts between the jaws clear of the parallels, a stop hole that the shortest Setup 4 overhang leaves over a jaw when the longest blank's whole allowance comes off that end, a Stage B strap off faced metal or in a cut, and a Y0 touch that the .200 edge finder cannot make clear of hold 1's right jaw side. P2 sits 13.51 from ZERO END, past the 6 in calipers, so its station is read on the DRO before unclamping. Setup 4's end cuts are conventional: the table moves away from you for the ZERO END cut and toward you for the length cut ([#418](https://github.com/pedropaulovc/prechips/pull/418) corrected the directions [#412](https://github.com/pedropaulovc/prechips/pull/412) had labelled conventional, which were climb).

The leadscrew-kit example (mm sheet) prints two pro pages or seven hobby pages (eight setup pictures): receive the purchased kit, saw the screw long, mill the cut end to length from the NEAR END, cut the five set-screw flats on the BS-0 dividing head (two per KP08 station at the sheet's 120° clocking; the handwheel flat, open through the cut end in two crossings, in its own 16 mm re-grip because at the cut-end KP08 flats' 42 mm stick-out it would bend about 0.09 mm against the 0.025 mm finish limit) and face the brass nut on the threaded offcut and countersink it. It declares its milling beams and cuts, so it renders with `milling_deflection` enforced. Its manifest has two members: the LeadScrew features bind the default STEP and the FeedNut features carry `configuration = "FeedNut"` with their own `member_steps` STEP; the adapter refuses any other member set. The nut runs on literal speeds because a plan has one `material_key` (the screw's).

The carrier example prints three pro pages or seven hobby pages from the v50 manifest (owner rulings 2026-11-13, blind review 2026-11-14: TOP alone is faced, and only when the sawn bar is over .480) and passes every check, `milling_deflection` included: each milling setup declares the straps that hold the plate against the cutter's lift (end milling, facing and the ends all strap over the end blocks), and each hole group the two strapped blocks that bracket it. The plate is 20.5 in long, so every milling setup is on the table, vise off, on four 1-2-3 blocks under strap clamps; on edge in the vise the jaws would grip at most 44.9 mm of the 152.4 mm-tall bar, under the one-third rule. The adapter refuses a block that could reach any hole, the HUB included, anywhere within its hand-placing tolerance (`block_tolerance_in`, measured from the scribed ZERO END); once any block key is set, all five (`block_inner_offset_in`, `block_outer_offset_in`, `block_length_in`, `block_width_in`, `block_tolerance_in`) are required. It also refuses a strap toe pad, centred `strap_toe_in` from the long edge and `strap_toe_width_in` across the plate (each requires the other; Pedro 2026-11-16: a toe-down step clamp, .25 in toe contact), that reaches past the blocks' span less that tolerance. Hole coordinates print from the lower-left corner as sheet 2's table does, all from one DRO zero; note 2's relations (B1, A2, B2, CL, CR, R1-R4 from A1 in X and the A1-B1 line in Y) print as their own table and are inspected before tapping by centring the DTI on a pin in each hole, Y corrected for B1's tilt of the line. The .3760-.3770 reamed bores are checked .376 GO / .377 NO-GO with the shop's pin-gauge set, after the 1 × 3 test coupon sawn with the blank (`coupon_width_in`, `coupon_length_in`) is spotted, drilled, reamed and gauged on two `coupon_parallel_width_in` parallels behind the plate (`coupon_hole_x_in`, `coupon_hole_y_in`). Once any coupon key is set, all are required; the coupon is placed by hand like the blocks, within `block_tolerance_in`, and the adapter refuses a coupon longer than the bar is wide, one that could reach the plate or a back strap block (over blocks 2 and 4, bound as `back_strap_blocks` for the holding text), and one too short for a parallel between each end and one hole diameter from its hole. The coupon's own strap (`coupon_strap_length_in`, the clamp body's length) runs along +X, heel toward the right as the holding text prints and the holes picture draws; from a toe at the coupon's right end its body must also clear that back strap block. It binds `coupon_hole_y_mm` for the envelope and `coupon_parallel_height_in`, the parallels at the blocks' `block_height_mm`; the holes setup's work top is the raw bar, `blank_thickness_mm`. Hobby draws the coupon in the saw and holes pictures. The bores' underside edges carry only the title block's edge break (no chamfer); the spacer's seat (`spacer_seat_dia_in`) is authored and the adapter refuses a broken edge that reaches it. The facing operation is authored as `steps`/`steps_detailed`, so both sheets print its caliper check and undersize stop as distinct lines.

Both jig parts draw from their adapter values (length, section, hole stations and row, hole or tap-drill size, thread zones) and authored holding parameters (jaw width and height, parallel thickness, overhang, edge-finder touch station, kerf). Each saw picture shows only that sheet's blanks (and, for a template with `data-offcut`, one `offcut_length_in` piece of the same bar named by it, as the feed block's notch spacer; for a plan with `coupon_width_in` and `coupon_length_in`, a strip that wide cut to the coupon, as the carrier's reamer test coupon); the bar beyond them is an unlabelled stub, so neither plan carries the other part's lengths. Jaws, parallels, cutters, taps and soft jaws are schematic symbols.

Every hobby picture draws from one palette ([`src/prechips/palette.py`](https://github.com/pedropaulovc/prechips/blob/main/src/prechips/palette.py)) by role: the same physical object looks the same in every part. Jaws, chuck jaws, soft jaws, straps and toe clamps are one clamp grey; 1-2-3 blocks, parallels, jacks and rests are dotted supports; cutters and edge finders are gold. Each role also differs by lightness, pattern or line style, so the pictures read in greyscale. The hobby traveler prints the palette's key once, after the tool list, listing only what its pictures draw (`<use>` and image elements count). Authored diagrams may keep their own CSS classes and ids; only classes that name a palette role are styled, and the palette's markers and patterns carry `prechips-` ids (an arrowhead is `url(#prechips-arr)`). Feed arrows show the cutter's path over the part and are labelled "cutter path"; on a knee mill the table moves the other way, and the setup text names that table motion. Coordinate axes and viewing-direction arrows are thin `axis` arrows, labelled where drawn and not in the key.

A fixture plate and its fence are `fixture-plate` (light, with vertical lines), distinct from the table and the vise body; edge clamps are `clamp`, like every other clamp, and the key calls that role "vise jaw, work holder" ("strap" and "clamp" are not in the key, since a part may be one, nor "hold-down", which a hardware screw is); supports are keyed "1-2-3, parallel, jack, stop". Tape or adhesive between a part and its plate is `bond` (pale, with staggered dashes), drawn under the arriving blank; a stop block on a plate is `fixture-support`; a screw, washer or plug that holds a part down through a hole is hardware (`part-rest`), not a clamp. Every callout leader ends on the feature and station its text names: a height stated on a centre line is called out on that centre line.

The shaft prints two pro pages or four hobby pages, one per setup. Hobby shows its short hold, supported turning, reversal and fit-scribed finishing setup, distinguishing cuts from the kept part and parted waste. Both domes use the [LMS 2569 ball/radius attachment](https://littlemachineshop.com/products/ball-and-radius-turning-tool-small) with its supplied HSS bit; hobby substitutes the attachment method in place of the compact instruction.

The shaft currently exceeds the default finish-bending screen on the last few millimetres of kept body cut before the follower engages (see Turning deflection), and its fit-scribe hold exceeds the unsupported stick-out limit (see Lathe holding). The explicit opt-outs above render its authored process unchanged; they are not a physics pass or machining approval.

Radius, height and cap limits come from CAD; frame-edge-to-tip is radius + 4.7625 mm per the [manufacturer's manual, pp. 4–7](https://littlemachineshop.com/cdn/shop/files/SmallRadiustoolinstructions.pdf?v=15026407504268274368). Jaw positions are set back for the attachment, with a 5 mm full-sweep gap required at setup. The retained 1400 rpm is an authored starting speed, not a supplier recommendation. Pictures share the instructions' CAD/process values; fixture symbols are schematic, not physical attachment models or collision checks.

Setups are numbered in order; operations use unique numbers in increments of ten. Operation tables align work instructions with tooling and cutting settings. Coordinate and inspection tables keep related values together.

### Archived-part comparisons

`examples/comparison/` condenses the archived brass ring, magic tube, T-nuts, cone pivot post, pivot bracket, pivot shaft and rocker-arm plans into the compact format:

```sh
uv run prechips traveler examples/comparison/demo-pivot-shaft.toml --profile examples/comparison/profile.toml --out out/pivot-shaft.html
```

These adaptations retain the archived process choices except the shaft's dome finishing, now using the LMS attachment rather than a stepped contour table. Source files are identified in each TOML header. The shaft, bracket and rocker bind dimensions to their adjacent feature-manifest snapshots; the remaining comparison plans use literal values. The comparison profile renders compact text only and explicitly omits stock-fit and turning-deflection enforcement. Archived longitudinal cuts without structured analysis data fail under built-in profiles rather than being silently accepted. The cone remains a built-up bonded-sleeve route, and the bracket retains the archive's illustrative dimensional intent.

At 10.5pt on Letter paper, the ring uses one page; the tube, T-nuts, bracket and shaft use two each. Pedro approved three pages for the cone and rocker so their remaining instructions stay readable. The cone's reusable fixture manufacture is a separate two-page `demo-cone-pivot-post-fixtures.toml` job.

## CAD dimensions

The examples read feature manifests under `examples/cad/`. Point `cad.file` at the corresponding source file directly to avoid maintaining another copy. Paths are relative to the plan. No STEP reader, FreeCAD, network access or hash checking is involved.

`cad.adapter` selects the part adapter and `cad.part` names the part the manifest must declare: an adapter refuses a manifest that is not in mm or whose `part` differs, so a plan pointed at another part's manifest stops before output. The adapters know no project: the plan supplies the part. The `examples/` plans are demos, and their manifests keep the CAD export format with demo identities (`base-bar`, drawing `DEMO-BASE-BAR`, and so on) and no source citations.

It applies only to names ending in `_mm`; on any other name it refuses. Every binding except `e` rounds its value to 9 places first (and prints a rounded −0 as 0), so unit-conversion float noise never reaches the sheet and one value prints the same digits under `.Nf`, `.Ndot`, `g`, `%`, `fraction`, `d` and bare: a bare `in` or `infraction` prints `9.525` mm as `0.375` or `3/8`, an adapter's `19.05 / 25.4` prints `3/4` under `fraction`, and `76.2` mm prints `3` under `ind`. `d` tests that rounded value, so it still refuses anything more than 5e-10 off a whole number, and `g` prints `0.1 + 0.2 - 0.3` as `0`. `e` takes the value as computed, so small magnitudes still print (`4e-10` under `.2e` is `4.00e-10`). See `examples/demo-base-bar.toml` for the complete recipe.

Each adapter applies the selected plan's setup frames and distinguishes model geometry from requirement limits. Named bindings update repeated dimensions and coordinate tables together. Conflicting machining targets and source acceptance limits are input errors. The shaft's reference span remains REF: its final length still follows the assembly fit. The rocker's hub-length aim is authored; its strap aim follows that aim and the bound face depths. Selected tools and gauges remain separate from source acceptance limits.

Changing CAD dimensions does not select new tools or redesign the machining process. Unsupported coordinate frames and conflicting cap geometry fail with an input error; general tool and gauge suitability is not checked. Literal-only plans work with a custom profile that omits `stock_fit`.

## Turning deflection

Both built-in profiles enforce `turning_deflection`, a narrow static bending screen for declared longitudinal turning of solid round stock. Default limits are **0.10 mm rough** and **0.005 mm finish** on the estimated **radial** bending at the tool; the diameter error left behind is about twice that, so the finish default corresponds to roughly 0.010 mm on diameter. These are authored limits, not guaranteed diameter error or handbook tolerances. A predicted exceedance or missing applicable data exits 2 before creating or replacing output. Successful checks print nothing on the traveler.

Declare `machine_kind = "lathe"` or `"other"` on every setup. Each lathe operation declares `cut_kind = "longitudinal_turning"` or `"other"`; facing, parting, grooves, attachment domes and other unsupported cuts use `"other"` and are outside this model, not checked passes. Longitudinal cuts require root `turning_material`, setup `turning_support`, and nonempty operation `turning_cuts` with explicit numeric geometry, DOC and feed. Printed material names and feed prose are not parsed. Numeric fields can use existing CAD/process bindings.

For numeric analysis fields, use exact whole-field bindings without formatting, such as `start_z_mm = "{{cad.body_cut_start_z}}"`; these preserve the underlying number and precision. Formatted bindings such as `:g` remain text and are refused where analysis requires a number.

Supports are `chuck_only` (`chuck_z_mm`), `tailstock` (also `centre_z_mm`), or `follower` (also `engage_z_mm`, `offset_mm`, `pad_width_mm`). Follower credit requires a toward-chuck traverse and a whole pad band on already-turned continuous cylindrical land produced by the declared pass; before engagement the model uses chuck/centre supports. It checks the declared pad footprint and both sides of engagement, not STEP gaps, actual pad contact/capacity or machine compliance.

Each cut declares `name`, `phase = "rough" | "finish"`, `start_z_mm`, `end_z_mm` (the full tool traverse), `kept_start_z_mm`, `kept_end_z_mm` (the retained feature inside that traverse), `start_diameter_mm`, `end_diameter_mm`, `max_radial_doc_mm`, `feed_mm_rev`, and `minimum_diameter_mm` (the smallest existing section elsewhere). An optional `max_deflection_mm` may tighten, but not relax, that cut's configured phase limit. The model conservatively bounds the declared maximum radial DOC at the final diameter, using the smaller of that diameter and the declared minimum section for bending stiffness. It does not assume a particular rough-pass order.

**Step turning toward the chuck.** A slender diameter turned to size in short axial steps from its free end, each step roughed and finished before the tool moves toward the jaws, is declared on the operation, not per cut:

```toml
turning_step = { length_mm = 2.0, load_diameter_mm = 6.35 }
turning_cuts = [
  { name = "Spigot rough", phase = "rough", start_z_mm = 30.7, end_z_mm = 12.92, kept_start_z_mm = 30.7, kept_end_z_mm = 12.92, start_diameter_mm = 6.35, end_diameter_mm = 1.6002, max_radial_doc_mm = 0.6096, feed_mm_rev = 0.0508 },
  { name = "Spigot finish", phase = "finish", start_z_mm = 30.7, end_z_mm = 12.92, kept_start_z_mm = 30.7, kept_end_z_mm = 12.92, start_diameter_mm = 1.6002, end_diameter_mm = 1.4732, max_radial_doc_mm = 0.0635, feed_mm_rev = 0.0254 },
]
```

`length_mm` is the longest axial step; `load_diameter_mm` is the smallest section between the jaws and the step being cut (the bar, or a diameter turned before). Every step takes each cut of the operation in order before the tool moves toward the chuck, so the cuts must form one stepped sequence: all share one traverse (`start_z_mm`/`end_z_mm`), the first starts from at least `load_diameter_mm`, and each later cut starts where the one before ended. A pass over a length already finished, or from a section thinner than the load, is not a step and refuses. The finished length outboard of the tool carries no cutting load, so every cut bends as a cantilever from the jaws to the tool: `load_diameter_mm` up to the step, then the step ahead of the tool, which earlier passes of that step have turned and a pass `t` deep meets at no less than `end_diameter_mm` + 2t (the worst pass depth up to the declared DOC is taken; a single finish pass meets its start diameter). The worst step is the farthest kept one. Rough and finish cuts are judged separately against their usual limits, so a declared step whose numbers fail still refuses. Step cuts must run toward the chuck (`start_z_mm` farther from `chuck_z_mm` than `end_z_mm`), on `chuck_only` support (a tailstock or follower would load the finished length), with `length_mm` no longer than the traverse; they take no `minimum_diameter_mm`. Both profiles print the step length as the operator's limit, rounded down: "Steps: at most 2.00 mm (.078 in), toward the chuck; rough and finish each step before the next." Operations without `turning_step` are unchanged.

With the packaged brass data, a Ø1/4 in bar 30.7 mm out of the jaws, a Ø.058 in spigot stepped back .70 in from the free end in .080 in (2.032 mm) steps estimates 0.091 mm rough (.024 in DOC, .002 in/rev) and 0.0049 mm finish (.0025 in, .001 in/rev); .10 in steps refuse the finish (0.0052 mm), and the same cuts without `turning_step` (the finished Ø over the whole span) refuse by more than ten times. [`examples/demo-step-pin.toml`](examples/demo-step-pin.toml) turns a Ø1/16 in spigot this way.

The limit applies over the kept range only, so sacrificial stubs, run-in in waste and parted-off ends are traversed but not held to it. Both bounds are required and must lie inside the traverse; when the whole traverse is kept, bind them to the same values as `start_z_mm`/`end_z_mm`. The screen cannot tell what is waste on its own and deliberately assumes nothing, so bind the bounds from CAD/process values rather than typing them. Support validation (in-span, no chuck crossing, follower band on turned land) always covers the whole traverse, and a refusal reports both the kept maximum and the whole-traverse maximum.

The supplied shaft binds its journal cuts to the kept journal (`north_apex_z` to `north_shoulder_z`, so the Ø5.7-bounded stub beyond the apex is excluded) and its body cuts to the longest body the fit-scribe can keep (`body_kept_end_z` = follower pad band − waste margin + dome height). The journal passes. The body finish still refuses at **Op 60**: the kept body reaches about 4 mm past follower engagement at Z152, and immediately before engagement the model estimates about 0.009 mm radial (≈0.018 mm on diameter) against the 0.005 mm limit and the 6.33–6.35 bearing band. That is a finding about the plan, not about waste: the follower engages after the last few millimetres of kept body have been cut on chuck/centre support alone, and the pad band is then set on a land turned under that deflection. The example retains its authored feeds and support geometry rather than tuning them to pass; the explicit opt-out above renders it unchanged.

Packaged [`turning.toml`](src/prechips/turning.toml) holds editable coefficients and source notes. Unit power comes from *Machinery's Handbook*, printed pp. 1085–1086 (corpus PDF pages 1406–1407); feed and wear factors are on printed p. 1087 (PDF 1408). Default material keys deliberately encode proxies, not measured stock properties:

It is a practical prototype, not a certificate or general machining-safety check.

Override only the values you need; a custom TOML merges partial tables over packaged defaults:


Unknown configuration keys, invalid numbers and feeds outside the factor table fail rather than extrapolate.

The configuration is read and validated once per render, before any check runs, so a broken override refuses the same way whichever check is enforced. When turning deflection, milling deflection and drill pecking are all disabled, the configuration is intentionally not read, even if a config path was supplied. Disabling a check adds no warning, checker status or certificate to the sheet.

## Milling deflection

Both built-in profiles enforce `milling_deflection` (`src/prechips/milling.py`), a static bending screen for every mill and drill-press cut, vise and table holds alike. Each setup declares how the part is held as a beam; each spindle operation declares its cuts. At every declared cut station the handbook cutting force bends that beam, and the worst station and bending axis of each cut is compared with its phase limit. A predicted exceedance or missing data exits 2 before creating or replacing output and names the setup, operation and cut, the estimated bending, the limit and the governing span. A pass prints nothing on the traveler. It runs with the declared-input checks (after `vise_holding`) and is disabled with `--disable-check milling_deflection`.

Default limits are **0.10 mm rough** and **0.025 mm finish**, authored, not handbook tolerances. The finish limit is one-tenth of the drawings' tightest general band (title-block .XXX ±.005 in, 0.254 mm wide), about .001 in. The rough limit matches turning and stays below the smallest authored finish allowance (.005 in, 0.127 mm), so the finish pass still cleans up. Per-cut `max_deflection_mm` may only tighten the phase limit.

Coverage, with no inference from prose:

- Every setup declares `machine_kind`. Lathe setups and their operations take neither `beam` nor `milling_cuts`; `turning_deflection` covers them.
- Every other setup declares `beam`: a table, or `"other"` only when none of its operations cuts with the spindle (saw, bench, hand tap, filing, inspection).
- Every operation with a `tool` declares `speed_kind`. A `speed_kind = "spindle"` operation declares `milling_cuts`: cut tables, or `"other"` only when the spindle removes no material (edge finder, indicator). Other operations take neither `milling_cuts` nor `beam`.
- A cutting operation may declare its own `beam`, which replaces the setup's table for its cuts: a re-grip, or a jack under a drill that a lifting end mill would not load.

```toml
beam = { axis = "x", hold = "cantilever", extent_mm = [0, "{{cad.blank_length_mm}}"], clamp_mm = "{{process.strap_x_mm}}", section_mm = { y = "{{cad.width_mm}}", z = "{{cad.blank_thickness_mm}}" } }
milling_cuts = [
  { name = "left end to ZERO END", kind = "side", phase = "finish", material = "aluminum_6061", cutter_dia_mm = 12.7, flutes = 2, axial_doc_mm = "{{cad.blank_thickness_mm}}", radial_woc_mm = 0.508, rpm = 1250, feed_mm_min = 152.4, stations_mm = 0 },
]
```

`axis` is the machine axis along the part; stations are millimetres along it in any frame the setup chooses, and `extent_mm` is the part's `[start, end]` in that frame. Holds:

- `cantilever`, with `clamp_mm` (a station or `[start, end]`: vise jaws, a strap pair, a chuck): a station inside the clamp does not bend; outside it is a cantilever from the nearer clamp edge, F·L³/(3EI).
- `between`, with `supports_mm = [a, b]`: two points that hold the part in the bending direction (straps, centres, a jack under a drill). Between them a pinned span, F·a²·b²/(3EI·l); beyond one, an overhang c, F·c²·(c + l)/(3EI). A block or jack only pushes; it is no support against a lifting end mill.
- `solid`, with `seat` (the rigid seat, stated) and `seat_mm`: a drill or plunge pushes straight down onto that seat (parallels under the hole, the table), no bending; a station off the seat refuses. A seat only takes a downward push, and every milling cut also loads the part across the spindle (side and slot cuts lift it too), so milling kinds refuse on a seat and need a cantilever or between beam. A hole between two parallels is not over a seat: it is a beam across them.
- `plate`, with `seat` (the plate, stated), `seat_mm` (the plate's `[start, end]` along the axis, which may run past the part), `clamps_mm` (two or more ascending stations on the part: edge clamps, toe clamps or straps that hold it down and against a fence) and `section_mm`: a thin part lying along x or y on a fixture plate. The plate takes every downward push (drill and plunge thrust, the end-tooth push of `end` and `face` cuts) with no bending; a station off `seat_mm` refuses. Only the clamps resist lift (the helix lift of `side` and `slot` cuts) and side load away from the fence, so both bend the part between clamp stations: a pinned span between the two clamps either side of the cut, an overhang beyond the end pair (the further clamps of a continuous part only stiffen it). Lift bends the thickness, side load the width.

A `plate` beam may add `tabs = { stations_mm, section_mm, length_mm }` for a part whose outline is cut through and which hangs on tabs to the clamped margin of its blank. That is usually an operation's own `beam`, with the part's `extent_mm` and `section_mm` in place of the blank's. `stations_mm` are two or more tab stations within `extent_mm`; the clamps then bear on the blank's margin, which may run past the part, so `clamps_mm` need only lie on `seat_mm`. `section_mm` is one tab's width along the beam and its thickness (`{ x, z }` for a beam along x) and `length_mm` its length across the slot (the cutter Ø). The tabs replace the clamps as the part's supports, and they give: each carries its lever-rule share of the load as a cantilever `length_mm` from the margin, L³/(3EI) plus shear 3.6·L/(E·A) (1.2·L/(G·A) at G = E/3, the least an isotropic material allows, since tabs are short), and across the slot it stretches, L/(E·A); the part moves with its tabs. Nothing else holds a cut-out part along its own length, so the cross-spindle force is also applied along the beam, shared equally by the tabs, and the beam axis becomes a loaded axis (a `finish_stock_mm` table names it too). Every tab station must be a clamp station, within 0.01 mm: a tab between clamps hangs the part on a margin strip that nothing holds down there, and the strip's bending is not modelled, so it refuses. Declare only tabs that join the part to a margin the clamps hold down; a fence holds nothing down.

```toml
# The blank on a sub-plate, four edge clamps pushing it against a fence:
beam = { axis = "x", hold = "plate", extent_mm = [0, 304.8], seat = "faced sub-plate", seat_mm = [-12.7, 317.5], clamps_mm = [38.1, 114.3, 190.5, 266.7], section_mm = { y = 38.1, z = 2.49 } }
# An operation after the outline is through, the part on four tabs:
beam = { axis = "x", hold = "plate", extent_mm = [6.35, 298.45], seat = "faced sub-plate", seat_mm = [-12.7, 317.5], clamps_mm = [38.1, 114.3, 190.5, 266.7], section_mm = { y = 16.0, z = 2.49 }, tabs = { stations_mm = [38.1, 114.3, 190.5, 266.7], section_mm = { x = 3.0, z = 0.5 }, length_mm = 3.175 } }
```

`section_mm` is the smallest section of the free span: the two sizes across the beam (`{ y, z }` for a beam along x; I = b·d³/12 with d along the bending axis), or `{ diameter }` for round work in a chuck or dividing head (I = π·D⁴/64). Solid holds take no section.

In a setup with a milling-vise `vise_hold` table or array, every `cantilever` beam, the setup's and each operation's, also declares `vise_left`: `"start"` or `"end"`, the extent end at the hold's left overhang (`overhang_mm[0]`), or `"other"` when the beam is not the hold's part lying along the jaws (upright or across the jaws). With `start` or `end` the extent may be no longer than `along_jaws_mm` (a machined part is shorter than the blank its hold describes), and `clamp_mm` must be the span the jaws grip, measured from the end whose overhang the hold declares (the left one when both are numbers) with the vise's `jaw_width_mm`, each within 0.01 mm; a setup with several holds names one with `vise_hold = N` (from 1). An operation's cantilever replaces the setup's beam for its cuts, so it is linked the same way (to a re-grip the setup's holds describe, or `other`). Beams in setups without a vise hold take neither field. Only a `start`/`end` link reads the hold, and only the hold it links, refused at that hold's own path (`setup N (…).vise_hold`); so `--disable-check vise_holding` leaves the other holds unvalidated.

A `plate` beam in a setup with a milling-vise hold links the hold that grips its plate: the only one, or `vise_hold = N`. That hold must declare `grips = "plate"` (see [Vise holding](#vise-holding)); a hold that grips the part contradicts a plate seat and refuses, and so does a `cantilever` whose `vise_left = "start"` or `"end"` links a plate-gripping hold. With `vise_hold = "other"` (the plate clamped to the table) the beam takes no `vise_hold`.

Each cut declares `name`; `kind` = `side` (periphery, `radial_woc_mm` below Ø), `slot` (full width, `radial_woc_mm` = Ø), `end` (end teeth produce a floor or face; width = stepover), `face` (face mill or fly cutter), `plunge` (centre-cutting end mill fed axially) or `drill`; `phase` = `finish` when the cut makes a surface or size the drawing holds (including drilled holes and tap drills), `rough` when a later cut removes it; `material`, a configured key, which a cut may omit when the plan declares `milling_material` (a kit whose members differ keeps it per cut; a cut naming its own key overrides the plan's); `cutter_dia_mm`; integer `flutes`; `feed_per_tooth_mm`, or `rpm` with `feed_mm_min` (feed per tooth = feed ÷ (rpm × flutes); hand-fed cuts declare the feed the operator applies; a cut `rpm` must equal the operation's numeric `rpm` when it has one, so a changed spindle setting refuses until the cut is updated). When the operation's shared setting calculates its feed from `chip_load_mm_per_tooth` and `flutes` (and the operation authors no `feed`), its cuts take both from the setting: a cut may omit them, must restate them exactly if it declares them, and may not declare `rpm` with `feed_mm_min`; literal feed text is never parsed. Such a cut is analysed at the larger of the chip load and the printed (rounded) feed ÷ (rpm × flutes), since the operator sets the printed feed. Each cut declares where it cuts, either `stations_mm` (one or more points: holes, a cut across the beam) or `traverse_mm = [start, end]` (a pass that travels along the beam, judged at its ends and at mid-span of a pinned span inside it, which bound every point between). Milling kinds also declare `axial_doc_mm` and `radial_woc_mm`; drills and plunges take neither. The loaded axes are not declared: a milling cut bends the beam in both axes across it, a drill or plunge only along the spindle (z), and on a beam along z (a part upright, drilled or plunged on its end) that thrust compresses the part and does not bend it, so no thrust data is read. Numeric fields accept exact whole-field bindings.

A rough cut may name the finish cut that follows it, `finish_cut` (the next `phase = "finish"` cut after it in the same operation, whose name is unique among that operation's cuts), with `finish_stock_mm`, one entry per loaded axis: the stock that finish cut leaves along it, or `"through"` where this cut passes through the part along that axis (an edge cutter whose tip runs below the underside passes through along z). Each axis is then compared with its declared stock instead of the rough limit, and a `"through"` axis is compared only with the cut's `max_deflection_mm`; a cut whose every axis is `"through"` must declare `max_deflection_mm`, or it would compare nothing and is refused; nothing is inferred from the finish cut's own fields. `max_deflection_mm` still only tightens, on every axis. Finish cuts take neither field and keep the finish limit.

The spindle is vertical (machine Z). Milling uses the handbook mean tangential force, unit power × removal rate ÷ cutting speed: Ft = 396000·Kp·C·W·w·d·ft·nt/(π·D) lbf in inches, where rpm cancels (printed pp. 1085–1088, PDF 1406–1409). C is the same feed-factor table as turning, read per tooth for milling; W is 1.10 for end and slab milling and 1.25 for `face` (the top of light and medium face milling). Along the spindle the force is Ft × the axial ratio, the helix lift of side and slot cuts or the end-tooth push of end and face cuts; across it the rotating resultant Ft·√(1 + `radial_ratio`²) is applied in each horizontal axis across the beam. The operation's tool (plan or shared shop record) may state `axial_ratio`, used for every milling kind (a face mill or fly cutter whose lead and rake push less), or `helix_deg` (above 0, below 90), giving tan(helix) for side and slot cuts only; a stated `axial_ratio` wins over the helix. Otherwise the configured `axial_ratio` applies, and a refusal names which source it used. A tool may also state `axial_direction`, the flutes' helix force on the part in side and slot cuts, whichever ratio applies: `"up"` (right-hand spiral, right-hand cut, the default: it lifts), `"down"` (left-hand spiral, right-hand cut, a downcut: it pushes the part onto its seat) or `"none"` (straight flutes: no helix force, the axial ratio taken as 0 for side and slot cuts; refused beside a `helix_deg`). State it from the tool's maker data; a downcut's force keeps its magnitude, so a cantilever or a between-supports beam bends under it as before. Both configured ratios are authored at 1.0: tan 45° covers the 30–45° helices of general-purpose and aluminium end mills when the helix is unknown, and the radial ratio matches turning. Drills and plunges use the handbook thrust T = 2·Kd·Ff·FT·B·W + Kd·D²·J·W (printed pp. 1090–1092, PDF 1411–1413) with standard-drill chisel factors (c/d 0.18) and W 1.30; a plunging end mill reuses it as an authored proxy. A drill or plunging cutter below Table 11's first row (1/16 in, such as a .062 miniature end mill) is checked at that row, an upper bound since FT and the chisel term rise with diameter; above the table it is refused. Beam cases are printed pp. 261, 264 and 265; sections pp. 239 and 255.

This is a stiffness screen at the cut, not predicted dimensional error. It uses the mean force over a revolution (intermittent tooth peaks are higher), ideal clamps and pins, and a uniform minimum section, and omits chatter, clamp slip, one-sided supports, cutter and machine compliance, torsion and thermal effects.

Instead of `clamps_mm`, `section_mm` and `tabs`, a `plate` beam may declare `bond = { shear_mpa, tensile_mpa, derating, pieces }`: the part stuck to the plate over its whole underside (double-sided tape, adhesive, wax). `shear_mpa` and `tensile_mpa` are the bond's strengths from its data sheet, `derating` (at most 1) the stated fraction of them the plan credits, and `pieces` everything the cut leaves on the plate: `{ name, kind = "part", area_mm2, width_mm }` or `{ name, kind = "waste", area_mm2 }`. A bonded part is backed everywhere, so nothing bends and no deflection limit applies (`max_deflection_mm`, `finish_cut` and `finish_stock_mm` refuse). Each milling cut's cross-spindle force must not exceed derated shear × area under every piece. The lift of a `side` or `slot` cut acts at a piece's edge: the stress there is taken as 4 × force ÷ lift area (a load at the edge of a rigid rectangular footing, F/A plus its moment), compared with the derated tensile strength. A part's lift area is a patch as long as the part is wide (`width_mm`², at most its area): a long strip does not resist a lift at one point with its whole length. Waste only has to stay on the plate, and its whole area counts. A downward push goes into the plate, a downcut tool's (`axial_direction = "down"`) included: its side and slot cuts lift no piece and bend nothing on a plate along z. An operation after the outline is through declares its own beam with the freed pieces. Screws, pins and bushings through the part are given no credit.

```toml
# A blank bonded to the plate; then the same hold once the outline is through:
beam = { axis = "x", hold = "plate", extent_mm = [0, 304.8], seat = "faced sub-plate", seat_mm = [-12.7, 317.5], bond = { shear_mpa = 0.69, tensile_mpa = 0.69, derating = 0.25, pieces = [{ name = "blank", kind = "part", area_mm2 = 11486, width_mm = 38.1 }] } }
beam = { axis = "x", hold = "plate", extent_mm = [6.35, 298.45], seat = "faced sub-plate", seat_mm = [-12.7, 317.5], bond = { shear_mpa = 0.69, tensile_mpa = 0.69, derating = 0.25, pieces = [{ name = "strap", kind = "part", area_mm2 = 4424, width_mm = 16.0 }, { name = "waste", kind = "waste", area_mm2 = 1372 }] } }
```

A bond check is a catalogue-strength screen: it omits peel at a piece's corner, the bond's dwell, surface condition, cutting fluid and heat except as the stated `derating`, tooth-impact peaks, narrow waste strips lifting locally, and anything a screw or pin adds. In `tests/test_milling.py` the strap-like flat bonded at 0.69 MPa derated to a quarter passes a .17 mm slot level under the freed 16 mm strap (0.139 of 0.1725 MPa) and refuses a .5 mm level (0.41 MPa).

A plate hold also omits clamp force (including the clamp force that passes through tabs and margin strips once an outline is through), tab and margin strength, the margin strips' own bending, plate and vise compliance, and lift at the edge away from the clamps: the part is a beam on its clamp line, and the fence holds nothing down. Toward the fence the whole edge carries side load; the screen takes the other direction.

`tests/test_milling.py` works a strap-like case: a 1018 flat 2.49 × 38.1 × 304.8 mm on a plate, four clamps 76.2 mm apart starting 38.1 mm from each end, and a 1/8 in 3-flute 30° end mill at .001 in/tooth.

- A face skim (2 in two-insert cutter, .0135 in deep, full width) passes: its push goes into the plate, and its 43.8 N side load bends the width 0.001 mm at the blank ends. The same cut refuses on a `solid` seat, which takes no side load.
- A .5 mm deep slot pass between the end clamps passes: 26.2 N lift (45.5 N × tan 30°) bends the thickness **0.024 mm** at mid-span against 0.10 mm rough. The full 2.49 mm in one pass lifts 131 N and refuses at 0.119 mm.
- The .5 mm pass run out to the blank end refuses: 38.1 mm past the last clamp nothing holds the end down, 0.143 mm.
- With the 16 mm wide outline on four 3 × 0.5 mm tabs at the clamps, a .2 mm finish pass (8.23 N lift) passes mid-way between tabs at 0.0248 mm (0.0178 mm part bending + 0.0069 mm at the tabs) against 0.025 mm, and refuses at the part end, 31.75 mm past the last tab: 0.100 mm.
- A tab at 150 mm, between clamps, refuses as not judged.

Keep those refusals; move clamps and tabs toward the ends or take lighter cuts, never tune the limits.

The former base-bar Setup 3 refuses (`tests/test_milling.py` keeps it as the positive control). With the bar strapped only at blank mid-length, squaring the left end (finish .020 in passes, 1/2 in two-flute end mill at 1250 rpm and 6 in/min, the full .5 in engaged) is a 221.5 mm cantilever: 26.6 N axial force bends it an estimated **0.057 mm** along Z against the 0.025 mm finish limit, and the right end's .010 in passes 0.029 mm. Squaring in the vise clamping instead, with the blank centred in the 158.67 mm jaws and faced to .47 in, leaves 142.1 mm cantilevers: 0.017 mm and 0.009 mm, a pass, which is what `examples/demo-base-bar.toml` Setup 3 now does. With `axial_ratio` = tan 30° (0.577) the strap value would still be 0.033 mm. Keep that refusal; do not tune ratios, limits or declarations to pass.

The `[milling]` section of packaged `turning.toml` holds the limits, ratios and wear factors; `[drilling]` the thrust tables (feed factors, diameter factors, chisel factors, wear). Override them like the turning values:

```toml

```

## Declared-input checks

v0.8 adds checks that read explicit structured plan fields and shared shop records. Both built-in profiles enforce every one after stock fit and turning deflection. A refusal exits 2 before output is created or replaced and names the check; a pass prints no checker text. Each key works with `--disable-check` and in a custom profile's `enforce` list.

Numeric fields are millimetres and accept exact whole-field bindings such as `"{{cad.bearing_dia}}"`. Setups name their machine with `machine_ref`, a key under `[machines]` in the shop file. Only shop records a plan references are validated; unknown fields in a referenced record are errors.

```toml
# In the shop file:
[machines.pm-30mv]
kind = "mill"                 # mill, lathe or dividing_head
rpm_ranges = [[100, 2800]]
travel_mm = { x = 584.2, y = 222.25, z = 355.6 }
spindle_to_table_mm = [60.96, 431.8]

[workholding.chuck-3jaw-6]
kind = "chuck"                # chuck or collet
grip_range_mm = [3.0, 140.0]  # or grip_sizes_mm = [...]
body_diameter_mm = 158.0
body_length_mm = 72.0

[gauges.micrometers]
range_mm = [0.0, 76.2]
resolution_mm = 0.00254

[limits]
stickout_ld_max = 4.0
tool_change_clearance_mm = 25.0
```

Lathes also take `swing_over_bed_mm`, `swing_over_cross_slide_mm` and `between_centres_mm`; dividing heads take `worm_ratio`, `plate_holes` and `direct_index_positions`. Workholding and gauge records may record ownership with `status` (see [Shared tools, stock and cutting settings](#shared-tools-stock-and-cutting-settings)); it is validated when a check reads the record but never printed, since the traveler does not list normal shop equipment.

### Cutting speeds

Key `cutting_speeds`. Every operation with a `tool` declares `speed_kind = "spindle"` or `"other"` (hand tapping, sawing, bench work). A `spindle` operation needs an rpm (authored, shared or calculated) inside one of its setup machine's `rpm_ranges`, inclusive; an `other` operation, or one without a tool, must not carry an rpm. Feeds, feed text and cutting performance are not checked.

Independently of enforcement, a shared setting may calculate its values. `sfm` replaces a literal `rpm` (never both): the operation supplies `cut_diameter_mm` and its setup a `machine_ref`; rpm = 12·sfm/(π·D_in), rounded to the nearest 50 with ties to even, clamped to the machine's lowest and highest speed, and dropped to the top of the range below when it falls in a gap. Instead of literal `feed`, declare `feed_mm_rev` (prints `0.05 mm/rev`) or `chip_load_mm_per_tooth` with `flutes` (feed = rpm × flutes × chip load, printed to whole `mm/min` or, with `feed_unit = "in/min"`, to 0.1 in/min). An authored operation `rpm` or `feed` still wins and is neither rounded nor clamped; a per-tooth feed uses the operation's resulting rpm.

```toml
[settings.shaft-finish-turn-hss-1018]  # shop file
tool = "shaft-hss"
material = "shaft-1018-cold-finished"
sfm = 110
feed_mm_rev = 0.05

[[setups.operations]]                  # plan; setup has machine_ref
speed_kind = "spindle"
tool = "hss"
settings = "shaft-finish-turn-hss-1018"
cut_diameter_mm = "{{cad.bearing_dia}}"  # 6.35 -> 1700 rpm
```

### Tool sizing

Key `tool_sizing`. Every operation with a `tool` declares `sizing`; an operation without a tool must not. Use `sizing = "other"` when no drawing size binds the cutter (saws, roughers, spot and pilot drills, taps, lathe tools), or a table:

```toml
sizing = { kind = "band", band_mm = "{{cad.bracket_dia_band_mm}}" }  # [low, high]
sizing = { kind = "tap_drill", tap = "tap" }                         # a plan tool id
```

A band passes when the operation tool's `diameter_mm` lies inside the inclusive band; a reversed band refuses. A tap drill passes when the operation tool's `diameter_mm` equals the named tap's `tap_drill_mm` within 1e-6 mm. Plan `[[tools]]` and shop `[tools.<ref>]` records take numeric `diameter_mm` and `tap_drill_mm` (never printed); shop values flow into the plan tool unless it sets its own. A missing diameter or tap drill, an unknown tap id or a missing `sizing` refuses. The base-bar adapter supplies each drawing limit as `<limit>_band_mm` (such as `bracket_dia_band_mm`, `counterbore_dia_band_mm`, `slot_width_band_mm`), plus `slot_cutter_band_mm` for its slot operation: the cutter cuts its Ø plus twice the adapter's path offset e, so the band is the slot width band less 2e, and a shop end mill other than the one the offsets assume refuses.

A band may add `depth_mm`, the cut depth the tool's flutes must cover: it refuses unless the tool declares a numeric `flute_length_mm` (never printed) at least that long, so a shank never follows its flutes into the hole (the feed block's 3/8 leadscrew plunges, against `block-em-3-8`'s recorded .50 in length of cut).

Only the selected tool is compared; no nearer tool is suggested. Thread designation, tap class, operation order, reaming allowance and actual cut size (runout, drill wander) are not checked.

### Indexing

Every setup declares `indexing`: `"none"`, or a dividing-head table naming a shop machine of kind `dividing_head`.

```toml
indexing = { head = "bs-0", tolerance_deg = 0.0795, angle_deg = 12.5182 }  # one setting
# indexing = { head = "bs-0", tolerance_deg = 0.05, angle_deg = 30, positions = 3 }  # open pattern
# indexing = { head = "bs-0", tolerance_deg = 0.05, positions = 7 }  # full circle, step 360/7
```

Candidates are direct indexing (`direct_index_positions`) and every `plate_holes` circle through `worm_ratio` (spaces = angle × ratio × holes / 360), in exact rational arithmetic on the authored decimals. An exact representation wins; otherwise the smallest one-step error, then direct, plate name and smaller circle. Each cumulative landing error, and for a full circle the closure against the nearest whole revolution, must be within `tolerance_deg`. The sheet prints the selection whenever a table is declared, independent of enforcement, as an `Index:` line such as `B 23-hole circle: 1 turn + 9 spaces; 12.5217° (target 12.5182°)`; an uncomputable table (unknown head, no plate data, neither angle nor `positions >= 2`) is an input error. Backlash, fixture tilt, locating the work and cutter access are not checked.

### Gauge capability

`gauge_capability` requires `gauge_checks` on every setup and operation that has `inspection` text, and refuses it where there is none. Use `gauge_checks = "other"` for non-dimensional acceptance (finish, fit-up, visual), or one entry per dimensional limit the inspection text states:

```toml
inspection = "Ø{{cad.bearing_dia_min:.3f}}–{{cad.bearing_dia_max:.3f}}; Ra ≤{{cad.bearing_finish:g}} µm."
gauge_checks = [{ gauge = "micrometers", band_mm = ["{{cad.bearing_dia_min}}", "{{cad.bearing_dia_max}}"] }]
```

`gauge` names a `[gauges]` record. Its `range_mm` must contain both ends of `band_mm`, and its `resolution_mm` must not exceed the band width (high − low); a one-sided MAX limit is the band `[0, max]`. Text that mixes dimensional and non-dimensional limits declares only the dimensional ones (the Ra above). Gauge kind, datum and reading method, check timing, GO/NO-GO pairs and roughness ranges are not checked, and the inspection text is not parsed.

### Lathe holding

`lathe_holding` checks each lathe setup's grip and unsupported stick-out. Every setup declares `machine_kind`; every lathe setup declares `lathe_hold`, either `"other"` (explicit opt-out, e.g. faceplate work) or:

```toml
lathe_hold = { chuck = "chuck-3jaw-6", grip_diameter_mm = 10.0, stock_diameter_mm = 10.0, stickout_mm = 15.0, minimum_diameter_mm = 10.0, support = "none" }
```

`chuck` names a `[workholding]` record with `kind = "chuck"` or `"collet"`; any other kind refuses. `grip_diameter_mm` must lie inside a chuck's inclusive `grip_range_mm`, or equal (within 1e-6 mm) one of a collet's `grip_sizes_mm`. `stock_diameter_mm` is the largest swung diameter and must be at least the grip and minimum diameters. With `support = "none"`, `stickout_mm` (jaw fronts to free end) must not exceed `limits.stickout_ld_max` × `minimum_diameter_mm`, the smallest diameter in the exposed span; equality passes. `support = "tailstock"` or `"steady_rest"` lifts that limit. A setup with `turning_support` must agree: `chuck_only` ↔ `none`, `tailstock`/`follower` → `tailstock`. Non-lathe setups must not carry `lathe_hold`. Not checked: jaw depth or clamping force, runout, supported bending, spindle-bore clearance, or whether the declared support is installed.

A step-turned hold (slender lengths turned in steps toward the chuck, see `turning_step`) adds `load_diameter_mm`, the smallest section between the jaws and every cut of the setup; the stick-out limit is then `stickout_ld_max` × `load_diameter_mm`. It needs `support = "none"`, `turning_support` `chuck_only`, and `minimum_diameter_mm` ≤ `load_diameter_mm` ≤ `stock_diameter_mm`. Every operation must then keep the load on that section, or the hold refuses: a `turning_step` operation must declare at least that `load_diameter_mm`, form one stepped sequence as `turning_deflection` requires (one traverse, the first cut from at least its load section, each later cut from where the one before ended) and run every cut toward the chuck (a return pass refuses); a plain turning pass is judged at the smaller of its end and minimum diameters (a pass over the finished length refuses); every other operation with a tool declares its own `load_diameter_mm`, the smallest section between the jaws and its cut, so a later chamfer or single-point thread on the finished length refuses. `load_diameter_mm` on an operation is refused outside a step-turned hold.

The shaft's first three setups pass (the reversed hold is 22 mm against 4 × Ø5.7 = 22.8 mm). The fit-scribe setup refuses: its stick-out follows the actual scribe, bound as the largest expected reading plus the 16 mm attachment-clearance jaw offset (`fit_stickout_max` = 17.33 + 16 = 33.33 mm), against 4 × Ø6.35 = 25.4 mm on the unsupported bearing. That is a finding about the plan; render it with `--disable-check lathe_holding`.

### Vise holding

`vise_holding` checks each milling-vise hold. Every non-lathe setup declares `vise_hold`: `"other"` (table clamps, saw, bench: no milling vise), a table, or an array of tables when the setup re-grips the part (refusals then name `hold N`). Lathe setups must not carry it.

```toml
vise_hold = { vise = "vise-pm-6", across_jaws_mm = 25.4, along_jaws_mm = 302.26, part_height_mm = 12.7, parallel_height_mm = 25.4, overhang_mm = [10.16, "rest"], jaw_offset = "centred", fixed_jaw_face = "as_bought", moving_jaw_face = "as_bought", moving_jaw_contact = "flat" }
```

`vise` names a `[workholding]` record with `kind = "vise"`, `jaw_width_mm`, `opening_mm` and `jaw_height_mm` (vise bed, where parallels sit, to jaw top); `bed_height_mm` (table to vise bed) is recorded but this check does not use it. `parallel_height_mm` is 0 for work on the bed. `overhang_mm` is `[left, right]` past the jaw sides; a negative value is the part end's inset inside the jaws. One side may be `"rest"`: along − jaw width − the other side. Two numbers must agree with jaw width and `along_jaws_mm` within 0.01 mm of authored rounding. `jaw_offset` is `"centred"` or `"balanced"`; a balanced hold adds `balance = "twin_part"` (an identical part mirrored at the other jaw end; the hold describes one of them) or `"spacer"` (a same-thickness block). Faces are `"machined"`, `"as_bought"` or `"sawn"`; `moving_jaw_contact` is `"flat"` or `"rod"`. Equalities pass, except that parallels must be strictly below the jaw top:

- `across_jaws_mm` ≤ `opening_mm`.
- `parallel_height_mm` < `jaw_height_mm`, strictly: parallels level with the jaw top leave nothing gripped, so equality refuses. Kurt: "select a size that keeps the bottom of the clamped part at or below the top of the movable and stationary jaws" ([DX6 manual](https://www.kurtworkholding.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Kurt-DX6_Manual_2024-05-30.pdf), p. 5).
- Grip depth min(part height, jaw height − parallels) ≥ `limits.vise_grip_min_mm` and ≥ `limits.vise_grip_fraction_min` × part height. The minimum guards a part standing out of the jaws: a part gripped over its full height (part height ≤ jaw height − parallels, its top at or below the jaw top) is fully held and passes it (Pedro 2026-11-16); the fraction rule still applies.
- `"centred"` requires the midpoint of the gripped span along the jaws to lie within the middle third of the jaw width: |midpoint − jaw width / 2| ≤ `limits.vise_centre_fraction_max` × jaw width / 2. Otherwise declare `"balanced"`. The one-third is a house rule; no source publishes a number. "Whenever possible, the workpiece should be clamped in the center of the vise jaws. However, when necessary to mill a short workpiece which must be held at the end of the vise, a spacing block of the same thickness as the piece should be placed at the opposite end of the jaws." ([TC 9-524](https://armypubs.army.mil/epubs/DR_pubs/DR_a/pdf/web/tc9_524.pdf), p. 8-16; Kurt DX6 p. 5 agrees.) A `"twin_part"` must lie wholly on one side of the centre, or its mirrored twin would overlap it.
- Overhang geometry feeds the two rules above only: overhang rigidity is not judged. No handbook, vise maker or training text gives an overhang limit, and the texts treat overhang past the jaw end as normal practice (Krar Unit 62; *Introduction to Machining* §9.11).
- A machined fixed-jaw face against an unmachined moving-jaw face needs `moving_jaw_contact = "rod"`: Krar, *Technology of Machine Tools* 6th ed., Unit 62 ("Place round bar between side 4 and movable jaw"), and [*Introduction to Machining* §9.11](https://workforce.libretexts.org/Sandboxes/a072766d-16cb-4dbc-9dd2-2f3c784c59e6/Introduction_to_Machining/09%3A_Manual_Vertical_Milling_Machines/9.11%3A_Block_Squaring). A rod between two machined faces is allowed: Krar says "not required", §9.11 "Do not use a round bar", Anderson & Tatro's *Shop Theory* (p. 309) still uses it. A sawn face against the fixed jaw is refused; that is a house rule with no published source.

`grips` is optional: `"part"` (the default) or `"plate"`, a fixture plate the part is clamped to. With `grips = "plate"` every size and face in the hold is the plate's (`part_height_mm` its thickness, `across_jaws_mm` its width in the jaws) and the same rules apply to it. The part on top is described by its `plate` beam (see [Milling deflection](#milling-deflection)); the clamps that hold it to the plate are not checked here.

The example shop declares `vise_grip_min_mm = 6.0`, Pedro's house choice: no published minimum exists for smooth jaws (Mitee-Bite says only that they need "much more than .060"" clamped). `vise_grip_fraction_min = 0.3333` is a common rule of thumb, not a standard: "Hold at least one-third of the part’s height in the jaws (more for tall, thin parts), and cut only the part that sticks out." ([Engineer Underground, "Workholding and Fixturing"](https://engineerunderground.com/resources/makers-fabrication/cnc-machining/workholding-fixturing/)). Training texts are stricter: "The parallels selected for a job should provide no more than ⅛" of extra material above the jaw plates" beyond the cut ([*Introduction to Machining* §9.5](https://openwa.pressbooks.pub/intromachining1/chapter/wa9-5/)), and "These parallels should only be high enough to allow the required cut" (TC 9-524, p. 8-15). A shop that wants that guidance can raise the fraction. `vise_centre_fraction_max = 0.3333` is Pedro's house rule (TC 9-524 says only that work held at the end of the vise needs a spacing block); both fractions must be above 0 and at most 1. *Machinery's Handbook* (27th ed.) has no vise guidance. Not checked: clamping force, jaw lift, cutting loads, overhang rigidity or deflection, part stability on the parallels, matched widths of twin parts, soft jaws or jaw depth.

The base-bar, end-bracket and feed-lever examples pass `vise_holding`. The base bar and feed lever fill the jaw width, so their centred holds sit 0 mm off centre against 26.44 mm allowed. The end bracket holds both brackets at once, one at each jaw end, each balancing the other (its saw operation takes both blanks from the same bar, adjacent, so their as-bought widths match in the jaws); on edge on 1 in parallels it grips 19.55 of its 25.4 mm (≥ 8.47 mm). Envelopes and jig pictures still read jaw and bed sizes from plan parameters that repeat the shop vise record; plan bindings cannot read shop records.

### Machine capacity

`machine_capacity` screens each setup against its `machine_ref` machine. Lathe setups use their `lathe_hold` (opt out with `lathe_hold = "other"`) and the chuck's `body_diameter_mm`/`body_length_mm`: max(stock Ø, chuck body Ø) ≤ `swing_over_bed_mm`, stock Ø ≤ `swing_over_cross_slide_mm`, and `stickout_mm` + chuck body length ≤ `between_centres_mm`. Every other setup declares `envelope`: `"other"` (saw, bench, no machine) or a table checked against a mill:

```toml
envelope = { x_span_mm = 469.9, y_span_mm = [152.4, 12.7], work_top_mm = [25.4, 12.7], deepest_cut_mm = [25.4, -2.032], longest_tool_mm = [6.0, 50.0], shortest_tool_mm = [6.0, 41.0] }
```

Each value is a number or an array summed as a stack, so bindings can name the layers (block + stock, holder gauge + projection, support height + tip Z below the underside). Heights are measured from the table and tool lengths from spindle nose to tip. X/Y spans are the cutter-centre travel the table must make and must not exceed `travel_mm`. Tool change above the work needs `work_top_mm + longest_tool_mm + limits.tool_change_clearance_mm <= spindle_to_table_mm[1]`; reaching the deepest cut needs `deepest_cut_mm + shortest_tool_mm >= spindle_to_table_mm[0]`. Equalities pass. Pairing the deepest cut with the shortest tool is deliberately conservative. Not checked: Z travel, fixture or jaw obstruction, clamps, toolpaths, collisions or fixture footprint on the table.

A part on a fixture plate adds the plate as a layer of the height stacks: `work_top_mm = [bed height, parallels, plate thickness, part thickness]`. The plate's footprint and weight on the table are not checked.

The base bar binds its stacks from CAD/blank sizes and process parameters: the stated ~18.5 in edge travel, 1-2-3 blocks lying flat (1 in), 1-3/8 in parallels, and archived inventory facts for the vise bed height and R8 collet/drill chuck gauge lengths. Its 1/2 and 5/16 in end mills have no archived length, so the plan bounds them by the neighbouring archived sizes, and its drills by the archived 13/32 in jobber. All base-bar and shaft setups pass.

### Drill pecking

`drill_pecking` (`src/prechips/pecking.py`) checks each twist-drilled hole's declared peck strategy against its depth over drill diameter (L/D). Every operation with a tool declares `drilling`: `"twist_drill"` for a twist-drilled hole on the mill, drill press or lathe tailstock, or `"none"` for everything else (spots, countersinks, reamers, end mills, taps, saws, turning tools). An operation without a tool declares none of these fields.

```toml
# A twist-drilled hole:
drilling = "twist_drill"
hole_depth_mm = "{{cad.thickness_mm}}"
peck = { step_mm = "{{process.rail_peck_mm}}", retract = "chip_break" }  # or "full"; or peck = "none"
```

```toml
# A spot, countersink, reamer, end mill or turning tool:
drilling = "none"
```

A twist-drilled hole declares `peck` and `hole_depth_mm` (bind it from CAD, or from the process value the plan already prints); the drill Ø is the operation tool's `diameter_mm`. `drilling = "none"` takes neither. With L/D = depth ÷ Ø, the hole refuses when:

- L/D exceeds `max_ld` (10): deep-hole tooling, not twist-drill pecking;
- the strategy is lighter than its band: above `chip_break_above_ld` (3) at least `chip_break`, above `full_retract_above_ld` (5) `full`;
- `step_mm` exceeds `step_max_diameters` (1) × Ø;
- it pecks at L/D ≤ `chip_break_above_ld` without a stated `peck_reason`, or states one where it justifies nothing.

Band edges and the step limit allow a relative 1e-9 of floating-point slack, so a hole of exactly 3 Ø in inch sizes (9.525 ÷ 3.175 mm) counts as ≤ 3.

The bands are rules of thumb, not standards: no peck at ≤ 3 diameters ([CNCCookbook, G81/G73/G83](https://www.cnccookbook.com/g81-g73-g83-drill-peck-canned-cycle/)); twist drills to 5 diameters, pecking from 5 to 7, parabolic flutes with pecking from 7 to 10 ([CNCCookbook, deep-hole drilling](https://www.cnccookbook.com/deep-hole-drilling/)); a peck of about one drill diameter, shortened as the hole deepens ([MTI, peck drilling cycles](https://www.mactool.com/atlas/drilling/peck-drilling-cycles/)). They live in the `[pecking]` section of packaged `turning.toml` and are overridden like the other sections. One band set serves every material: the sources name long-chipping (aluminium, steel, stainless, copper) and short-chipping (cast iron, brass) materials but give no numeric shift, so no material is read. The parabolic-flute advice above 7 diameters is guidance, not a requirement, and is not checked (no tool field states flute form): the end bracket's 7.4-diameter holes take a conventional-flute jobber #29 with full-retract pecks. Chip evacuation, coolant, a shortening step and end-mill plunges are not checked either. Plans state the peck in their instruction text: bind the step from one process parameter in both places, `step_mm = "{{process.x_peck_mm}}"` and `every {{process.x_peck_mm:in.3dot}}`, as the examples do (one drill Ø per peck).

Every jig-part example passes. Their drilled holes: riser #30 through .24 (L/D 1.9) and feed lever 11/32 through .50 (1.5), no peck; carrier CL/CR #29 through .47 (3.5), chip-break; base-bar #12 through .47 (2.5), no peck; base-bar rail #29 through .47 (3.5, full retract as the CAM program drills them, heavier than its chip-break band), carrier #43 through .47 (5.3), pivot/feed-block #15 through .96 (5.3) and end-bracket #29 through 1.000 (7.4), full retract; pivot/feed-block F tap drill through .96 (3.7), chip-break; feed-block 5/16 pilot .90 deep (2.9; `hole_depth_mm = "{{process.lug_bore_depth_mm}}"`, printed as `{{process.lug_bore_depth_mm:in.2dot}}`), no peck. The pivot-shaft comparison declares `drilling = "none"` on every tooled operation (its centre drill is a spot).

## Profiles

| Preset | Setup diagrams | Operation instructions | Enforcement |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `pro` (default) | Omitted; diagram files are not opened | Compact `instruction` or `steps`, one line per step | Stock fits; turning and milling meet bending limits; every declared-input check passes |
| `hobby` | Included for every setup, plus any operation diagrams | `instruction_detailed` or `steps_detailed` when present, otherwise the compact slot; steps spaced apart | As `pro`; every setup has a readable SVG diagram |

Enforcement is limited to the declared stock, setup-diagram, static longitudinal-turning, static milling/drilling-bending and declared-input contracts. It does not establish machining safety. The diagrams illustrate setups using CAD-derived part dimensions and authored fixture layouts; no FreeCAD is needed.

Use `--profile path/to/profile.toml` for a custom profile:

```toml
name = "illustrated"
features = ["setup_diagrams", "detailed_instructions"]
enforce = []  # Explicitly skip every check; allow missing diagrams.
```

Add `"setup_diagrams"` to `enforce` to require one for every setup; it must also appear in `features`. Add `"stock_fit"` to require CAD and fitting blank dimensions independently of diagrams; add `"turning_deflection"` to enforce the declared longitudinal-turning screen. These and the declared-input keys (`cutting_speeds`, `tool_sizing`, `indexing`, `gauge_capability`, `lathe_holding`, `vise_holding`, `milling_deflection`, `machine_capacity`, `drill_pecking`) are the supported enforcement keys. Feature keys are `setup_diagrams` and `detailed_instructions`: custom profiles must explicitly enable the latter to select detailed operation text or steps (and the spaced step layout), regardless of profile name or diagram selection. Unknown keys are errors.

## Shared tools, stock and cutting settings

Plans can reference one shop: a file, or a directory whose `*.toml` files (directly in it, not in subdirectories; names starting with `.`, such as editor or `._` resource-fork files, are skipped) are merged by section (`tools`, `stock`, `settings`, `machines`, `workholding`, `gauges`, `limits`, ...). A record key, a `limits` key or a top-level value defined in two of the directory's files is refused, naming both files; everything else is read as one file would be. The output may not be a `.toml` file in a referenced shop directory. The base-bar, end-bracket, feed-lever, shaft and bracket examples use `examples/shop.toml`; the T-nut example uses the split directory `examples/comparison/shop-t-nuts/`. Mixed-tool operations and unspecified settings remain inline.

```toml
# In the plan, before any tables:
shop = "../shop.toml"  # Relative to this plan: a file or a shop directory.
material_key = "shaft-1018-cold-finished"
stock_refs = ["rod-1018-quarter"]  # Optional; keys under [stock] in the shop file.

[[tools]]
id = "blade"
ref = "shaft-blade"
name = "Blade"  # Optional local override.

[[setups.operations]]
tool = "blade"
settings = "shaft-relief-1018"
instruction = "Plunge south relief to Ø5.70."
# rpm = 1050  # Overrides only rpm; shared feed remains.
```

```toml
# In the shop file:
[tools.shaft-blade]
description = "M2 square blade"
status = "to_order"  # Optional: "on_hand", "not_received", "to_order" or "to_make".

[stock.rod-1018-quarter]
name = "1/4 in 1018 rod"  # Optional.
description = "1018 cold-finished round, 1/4 in × 36 in"
status = "on_hand"  # Optional, as for tools.

[settings.shaft-relief-1018]
tool = "shaft-blade"
material = "shaft-1018-cold-finished"
rpm = 1200
feed = "0.02 mm/rev"
```

Tool `name`, `description` and `status` overrides are independent, as are operation `rpm` and `feed` overrides. A referenced setting must match the tool's shared identity and the plan's `material_key`, even when both cutting fields are overridden. These identities are explicit; printed material descriptions are not parsed.

A shop `[tools.<key>]`, `[gauges.<key>]`, `[workholding.<key>]` or `[stock.<key>]` record may record ownership with `status`: `"on_hand"`, `"not_received"`, `"to_order"` or `"to_make"` (a shop-made item not yet made); any other value is refused, and an absent `status` means not recorded. A plan names the stock it needs with `stock_refs`, a nonempty array of `[stock]` keys; it requires `shop`, and unknown or repeated keys are refused. A stock record takes `description` (required), `name` and `status`, nothing else. The plan's `material` and `stock` text stay required and remain what the sheet prints. A tool whose status is not `on_hand` is marked beside its tooling-list entry, **(to order)**, **(not received)** or **(to make)**, and each such referenced stock record adds **(to order: name)**, **(not received: name)** or **(to make: name)** to the stock line (its `description` when it has no `name`). `on_hand` and an unrecorded status print nothing, so those sheets are unchanged. Gauge and workholding status is never printed.

Shared strings are literal. Plan-owned overrides retain CAD/process bindings. Missing references, mismatched identities and invalid selected records fail before output. Inline-only plans remain supported, and no shared input file (each `*.toml` of a shop directory) can be used as the output. This is an authored library, not tool selection; the only calculation is a setting's declared surface speed and feed rate (see [Cutting speeds](#cutting-speeds)).

## Process TOML

Dimensions and manual feed instructions are strings with explicit units; spindle speeds are positive integer rpm. Tool identifiers connect operations to descriptions and are not printed.

```toml
title = "Part setup sheet"
part = "drawing-part-name"
revision = "drawing revision"
material = "drawing material"
stock = "stock dimensions and quantity"
note = "Optional job-specific prerequisite"

[[tools]]
id = "cutter"
name = "Short tool name"
description = "Tool description and size"

[[setups]]
title = "Operation and setup"
machine = "Machine"
machine_kind = "other"  # Use "lathe" for lathe setups.
holding = "Workholding, orientation and necessary clearance"
datum = "Datum faces, axis directions and zero"
inspection = ["Setup-level inspection and acceptance limits"]
diagram = { file = "diagrams/setup.svg", alt = "Workholding and datum orientation" }

[[setups.operations]]
tool = "cutter"
instruction = "The cut, dimensions and coordinates"
# instruction_detailed = "Optional method detail, replacing the compact instruction"
# Or, in place of instruction / instruction_detailed:
# steps = [{ kind = "action", text = "One step" }, { kind = "check", text = "A check" }]
# steps_detailed = [{ kind = "stop", text = "A condition that stops the job" }]
# rpm = 1000              # Supply the actual authored spindle setting.
feed = "Authored feed with units, or a manual-feed instruction"
inspection = "Operation-specific inspection and acceptance limits"
# diagram = { file = "diagrams/stage.svg", alt = "A stage of this setup" }  # Optional.

[setups.operations.table]
columns = ["Feature", "X", "Y"]
rows = [["Feature name", "Authored X coordinate", "Authored Y coordinate"]]
```

Root `note`, tool `name`, setup `holding`, `datum`, `inspection`, and operation `tool`, `rpm`, `feed`, `inspection` and `table` are optional. Omit irrelevant setup fields rather than filling them with boilerplate. Omit `tool` for inspections or work requiring only ordinary shop equipment. Tool names provide short labels in operation tables; the tooling list retains full descriptions. Other shown fields are required, except that an operation gives `instruction` or `steps` (below); tools, setups and operations must be nonempty. Optional operation tables contain string `columns` and string-array `rows`, each matching the column count. Unknown fields and unresolved tool references produce an input error. Repeat tables in working order; each operation belongs to the most recent setup. Setup inspections print after its operations. Do not invent unknown machining values to fill a field.

An operation's optional `instruction_detailed` must be a nonempty string. With `detailed_instructions` enabled it replaces, never appends to, `instruction`; operations without it retain their ordinary instruction. Put a specific hobby method here rather than repeating the compact paragraph or adding generic tutorials. Both variants share tooling, settings and inspection limits.

An operation's work has two slots, each holding text or steps, never both: the compact slot is `instruction` or `steps` (one is required), the detailed slot `instruction_detailed` or `steps_detailed` (optional). The slots pair independently, so compact text may sit beside detailed steps. `steps` is a nonempty array of `{ kind, text }` tables; `kind` is required and is `"action"`, `"check"` or `"stop"`. Steps print as a numbered list: a check line carries a bold **Check:** label and a thin rule, a stop line a bold **Stop:** label, bold text and a heavy rule, so they read apart in greyscale. The label comes from `kind`: do not repeat it in `text`. Pro starts each step on its own line with no extra spacing; with `detailed_instructions` enabled, steps are spaced apart.

Instruction and step text may use basic markdown: `**bold**`, `*emphasis*` and `` `code` `` (code spans are literal). Emphasis needs a letter or digit just after the opening `*`, and never closes right after `(`, so footnote marks such as `(*)` print as typed; it may close after other punctuation, as in `*Note:*`. In `instruction` and `instruction_detailed`, whole lines starting `1. ` (any number) become a numbered list, and whole lines starting `* ` (asterisk, space) a bulleted list. A numbered list starts at its first number; a later number that jumps ahead prints as written (`10.`, `20.`), and a repeated or lower one counts on, so `1.`, `1.`, `1.` prints 1, 2, 3. A line starting `- ` never makes a list: it always prints as typed, so tolerances such as `+0.05` over `- 0.02 mm`, or stacked limits `- 0.013` over `- 0.028`, keep their minus signs. A blank line between text and a list, or between two lists (numbered or bulleted), prints as a blank line and starts a new list. Text is HTML-escaped first, then marked up, so authored HTML prints as text. Strings without markup render exactly as before, and the steps and markup styles are added to a sheet only when it uses them.

`diagram` is optional in the process schema, on a setup and on an operation, in the same `{ file, alt }` shape. Its `file` and `alt` strings are required when it is supplied. SVG paths are relative to the process TOML, regardless of the working directory. An enabled diagram must parse as SVG; it is embedded as an image, not executable page markup. In `pro`, unused diagram files are not opened.

A setup has at most one `diagram`, printed under its heading. An operation's `diagram` prints just before that operation's row, grouped with it so a page break cannot separate them, for a later stage of the same clamping (a hole layout after facing, say). Operation pictures print wherever setup pictures do (`setup_diagrams` in `features`) and add their roles to the picture key; a profile's required-diagram rule (`setup_diagrams` in `enforce`) asks for the setup's picture only, which an operation picture cannot replace.
