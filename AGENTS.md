# Prechips prototype

Prechips restarted at **v0.10**. The current milestone is **v0.11 oblique-jig parts and milling deflection** (package 0.11.12): the `milling_deflection` check and the remaining demo jig parts. It remains a prototype until Pedro finds it useful, not a machining certification system.

## Scope and milestones

- Develop in small, usable milestones. Pedro approves each milestone. Never work more than three milestones ahead of his last review.
- The examples are demos (`examples/demo-*.toml`, owner ruling 2026-11-16): they do not mirror any consuming project's plans, and prechips knows nothing about its consumers. Source, examples, tests and docs name no consumer's parts, drawings, files or issues; adapter keys describe the part (`base_bar`, `end_bracket`, ...), and the expected manifest `part` comes from the plan's `cad.part`, never from code. Demo manifests keep the CAD export format with demo identities (`DEMO-*` drawing numbers) and no source citations.
- v0.10: a machine-readable manual-manufacturing process in TOML and a simple CLI producing printable traveler HTML for a base bar (now the `demo-base-bar` example).
- v0.2: `pro` and `hobby` presets plus custom TOML profiles. Enforcement is limited to setup-rendering completeness. Hobby includes practical authored SVG setup diagrams and selects optional operation `instruction_detailed` in place of `instruction`; custom profiles opt in with `detailed_instructions`. No CAD/FreeCAD requirement. Pro is the default.
- v0.3: bind dimensions, drawing identity and diagram feature geometry to the selected CAD/drawing export. Keep process choices explicit. Prefer released data over importing CAD code or reconstructing dimensions from drawings.
- v0.4: both built-in profiles compare declared rectangular blank dimensions with the CAD nominal envelope in explicit length/width/thickness correspondence; custom profiles may omit `stock_fit`. No automatic stock rotation, parent-bar yield, machining allowance or machining simulation. Successful checks add nothing to the sheet.
- v0.5: explicit part adapters bind finished dimensions, setup coordinates and inspection limits from the selected feature manifests. Keep source geometry and requirements separate from authored tooling, allowances, fit-to-assembly choices and gauges. The bracket manifest remains illustrative example intent, not a released dimensioned drawing. These adapters do not imply generated setup pictures.
- v0.6: plans may explicitly reference one shared shop TOML for tool descriptions and named material/tool cutting settings. Local fields override shared fields individually. Do not infer identities, calculate recommendations or parse settings out of operation prose.
- v0.7: built-in hobby supports the pivot shaft's four CAD-manifest-driven setup elevations. Keep arriving material, active cuts, datums and support orientation consistent with the plan; fixture symbols are schematic. Round-stock diameter covers nominal shaft geometry, and available length covers the authored sawn-blank demand, not a fixed fit-to-assembly finish length. No native CAD engine or general setup simulation.
- v0.8: declared-input checks read explicit structured plan fields and shop records (`machines`, `workholding`, `gauges`, `limits`); no geometry engine. Inputs are expected to be written by an AI agent and comprehensive: built-in profiles require every applicable setup and operation to declare each check's data or an explicit opt-out value. Use shop and plan values as written; do not add measured/unmeasured provenance. Each check is one module registered in `checks.py`, refuses before output, prints nothing on a pass and has its own `--disable-check` key. Calculated speeds, feeds and index settings print in place of authored values; an authored value still wins.
- v0.8 keys: `cutting_speeds`, `tool_sizing`, `indexing`, `gauge_capability`, `lathe_holding`, `machine_capacity`; `stock_fit` also covers the bracket and rocker CAD nominal envelopes against their sawn flat-bar blanks. The shaft's fit-scribe hold refuses `lathe_holding` (33.33 mm unsupported against 4 × Ø6.35 = 25.4 mm). Keep that refusal and its documented opt-out, as with Op 60; do not tune jaw offsets or limits to pass.
- v0.9: the base bar reads the v48-onward manifest (v50 now; bar 1 alone carries the #12 bracket holes; rail stations carry one ±0.05 mm band and are drilled at their printed stations, the untrimmed rail fitted and trimmed flush afterwards, per owner ruling 2026-11-16, which supersedes the R4 transfer from each bar's own rail). `end_bracket` binds the end bracket's two shared-axis #8-32 holes and their per-face thread zones and tap-tip marks; `feed_lever` binds the feed lever's envelope, two clearance holes and (v0.11) its `grip_corners` fillet. `examples/demo-end-bracket.toml` and `examples/demo-feed-lever.toml` transcribe the 2026-11-02 hand travelers. The feed lever's holes are the 11/32 drill, Ø.3438 +.004/0 (owner ruling 2026-11-13), so its plan passes `tool_sizing` with no opt-out. No backwards compatibility with older manifests; when a release changes the drawing, follow the manifest and record what changed.
- Base bar, bar 2: drawing sheet 2 swaps the rail and table seats, and the manifest (v48 onward) describes bar 1 only. Keep the per-bar face marks (bar 1 C'BORE SIDE, bar 2 RAIL SIDE), datums, rock test and inspect picture authored from the two sheets until the manifest exports bar 2. The 1/2 in end mill alone cuts the slots on the adapter's cutter-centre rectangle (offset e sideways and e past each arc centre); a plain side offset makes a short slot and must not be authored.
- The end-bracket adapter's thread zones and tap-tip marks are the single deliberate exception to "no values from prose": v48 onward states them only in drawing note text, so they are read with exact patterns and refused on any rewording or inch/mm disagreement. Do not add further prose parsing; replace this one with structured `thread_zone`/`tip_mark` fields once the CAD export provides them.
- v0.9 hobby: built-in hobby renders both jig parts with one `jig-*` picture per setup (`src/prechips/jig_diagrams.py`; the templates under `examples/diagrams/{demo-end-bracket,demo-feed-lever}/` are bare roots, the shell is generated). Part geometry comes from adapter values; jaws, parallels, overhangs, edge-finder stations and kerf from authored parameters; fixture symbols are schematic. Each saw picture shows only its own sheet's blanks, so neither plan carries the other part's lengths. Keep each picture's holding, datum and active face consistent with its setup text, and keep hobby `instruction_detailed` to the hand sheets' part-specific methods (Y0 on the fixed jaw, tap tape flags, corner filing).
- v0.11 leadscrew kit: `leadscrew_kit` binds the v50 two-member manifest (LeadScrew and FeedNut); its hobby pictures are the `kit-*` layouts in `src/prechips/kit_diagrams.py` on the jig shell.
- v0.10 vise holding: `vise_holding` (`src/prechips/vise.py`) requires `vise_hold` on every non-lathe setup (`"other"`, a table, or an array for re-grips) against a `kind = "vise"` workholding record: opening, parallels below the jaws, grip ≥ `limits.vise_grip_min_mm` (Pedro's 6 mm house choice; a part gripped over its full height, top at or below the jaw top, passes it: Pedro 2026-11-16) and ≥ `limits.vise_grip_fraction_min` × part height (one-third, a cited rule of thumb, not a standard), centred (gripped-span midpoint within `limits.vise_centre_fraction_max` × half the jaw width of centre: the middle third, a house rule) or balanced (`balance = "twin_part"` or `"spacer"`; a twin must clear the jaw centre), rod against an unmachined moving-jaw face, no sawn face on the fixed jaw (house rule). Overhang is declared geometry only; no source gives an overhang limit, so rigidity is not judged. The end bracket holds both brackets per clamping, one at each jaw end (X0 left INNER END, XR right). Do not invent limits or retune holds to pass. Envelopes and jig pictures still read vise sizes from plan parameters that repeat the shop record.

- v0.11 carrier: `carrier` (`src/prechips/cad_carrier.py`) binds the carrier from the v50 manifest; its pictures are `src/prechips/carrier_diagrams.py`, unioned into the layout lookup in `cad_diagrams.render_cad_svg` beside `JIG_LAYOUTS`. Every milling setup is table-clamped on four 1-2-3 blocks (the plate is 20.5 in); the adapter refuses a block that, anywhere within `block_tolerance_in` of its station, could reach any hole, HUB included, and requires all five block keys once any is set. `strap_toe_in` and `strap_toe_width_in` each require the other; the whole toe pad must sit inside the blocks' span across, shrunk by `block_tolerance_in` (Pedro 2026-11-16: toe-down step clamp, .25 in toe contact). Hole rows print in sheet 2's lower-left-corner frame; relation checks use the manifest's A1-relative bands. The drawing has no underside pin chamfer, only the edge break; the spacer seat (`spacer_seat_dia_in`) is authored. The reamer test coupon's `coupon_*` keys are all-or-none and checked in the adapter (placed within `block_tolerance_in`: behind the plate, clear of the back strap blocks 2 and 4, room for a `coupon_parallel_width_in` parallel each side of its hole; its own `coupon_strap_length_in` strap body, heel toward +X as the holding text prints and the picture draws, from a toe at its right end clear of block 4's back strap; `coupon_hole_y_mm` and the parallels' height are bound, never authored), so pro and hobby refuse alike; the pictures only draw it (saw: `jig-cutlist`'s coupon strip; holes: on its parallels). Beam stations for `milling_deflection` (`end_block_x_mm`, `inner_block_x_mm`, `block_span_mm`, `end_block_blank_x_mm`, hole-group `*_x_mm`) come from the adapter, in mm from the finished ZERO END (blank frame for the `_blank_` pair).
- v0.11 carriage blocks: `pivot_block` and `feed_block` (`src/prechips/cad_carriage_block.py`) bind the pivot and feed blocks from their v49 manifests: envelope, the flat-floored pivot seat and its tap, the counterbored 2 × 2 hole table from each sheet's own row side, and the feed block's lug, leadscrew clearance (a plain through hole) and nut taps (assembly-transferred: refused unless `at_assembly`, never machined in the traveler). Their `jig-block-*` pictures live in `src/prechips/block_diagrams.py`, in the jig shell.
- Arc strap: `arc_strap` (`src/prechips/cad_arc_strap.py`) binds a flat strap between two concentric arcs from its v50 manifest and, from authored parameters, places it in a blank held on a fixture plate (`beam.hold = "plate"`, `vise_hold.grips = "plate"`): X from the plate's end stop, Y from the fence. Plan parameters are numbers, so clamps are `clamp_N_x_mm`. The faces and the hub hole are cut under edge clamps; the outline is cut with the blank bonded to the plate and screwed down through the hub hole (no tabs: the adapter binds none). For that stage it binds the slot depths above and through a skin, the fence notches and the bonded areas a `beam.bond` needs, and refuses a slot that reaches the fence or breaks out of the front edge, a breakthrough that reaches the plate through the tape, notches that do not span the stretch where the fence-side strip is under `sliver_width_min_mm`, a hold-down flange within `bushing_clearance_mm` of the strap's edge, and a drill or reamer tip within `relief_floor_clearance_mm` of the floor of the hole in the plate. `hub_relief_dia_mm` needs `x_stop_tolerance_mm` and is refused when the hole leaves less than it round the hub hole. The plate, its fence, end stop, plug and screw are fixtures made beforehand; the demo cuts only the strap. Its `plate-strap` pictures are `src/prechips/strap_diagrams.py` (steps `saw`, `rough`, `finish`, `hub`, `outline`, `inspect`). A printed inspection value must be what the stated instrument reads: the corner and foot stations run along the strap, so text and pictures that gauge them from the hub with calipers use the bound slants (`corner_slant_*`, `foot_slant_*`), never the station bands. The demo judges the rough slot, the finish pass and the through cut on the strap alone (a thin skin under the slot carries no lift across it), and only the fence sliver, cut first, on the whole blank. Its depth levels are the ones the bond check accepts; do not deepen a level, raise the tape strengths or the derating without a source. Drill, ream and plunge feeds are one `*_feed_per_min_mm` parameter each, bound into both the printed feed and the cut's `feed_mm_min`; the arc radii and tip-land angle are the program's path and are printed as not gauged.
- Fence plate: `fence_plate` (`src/prechips/cad_fence_plate.py`) binds a fixture plate with an integral fence and end stop from its v51 manifest, in the plate's own work-zero frame (stop face X0, fence face Y0, plate top Z0). It reads only drawing requirements: the plate's ends must be exported as `left_end`/`right_end` faces (never infer a stock length from a notch bound) and the hub position bands must be listed requirements. It generates no pictures; `examples/demo-fence-plate.toml` ships authored palette SVGs, and its cut widths, depths and feeds are authored values from a measured setting pair, not to be changed to pass a check.
- Hex block: `hex_block` (`src/prechips/cad_hex_block.py`, pictures in `hex_diagrams.py`) binds a hex-bar block with a corner-to-corner cross bore and an axial tapped hole in each end. The tap reach it binds is the tap POINT's depth (full thread plus the plug tap's lead), capped at the least drilled depth so a tap run to the flag cannot bottom. The corner-down cross hold is geometry, not a flat-seated vise hold: plans declare `vise_hold = "other"` and the adapter binds where the corner, axis and top corner sit from the parallel thickness; it refuses parallels under the bore because the drill exits there.
- Clamp half: `clamp_half` (`src/prechips/cad_clamp_half.py`, pictures in `clamp_diagrams.py`) binds either half of a split clamp block round a column, told apart by its features (the front's clearance ears, the back's tapped ears). The pair is bored screwed together with the front's mating face on the bore axis, so the back is sized deeper by its nominal grip offset (`pre_face_depth_*`) and faced back after the bore; a manifest describes one half only, so the plan states the other half's bored depth band (`other_half_depth_min_mm`/`_max_mm`). Every wall and hold check is judged at the band extremes, never at nominals. The adapter refuses holds that cut into their supports or hardware: parallels against the jaws reaching the ears (a tapped ear's edge is its thread's basic major diameter, from the numbered designation), parallels under the pair reaching the bore (the back's may stop short of it, `back_parallel_end_mm`), a screw reaching the back face of the thinnest pair and a block between the screw heads that touches them.
- Drilled block: `drilled_block` (`src/prechips/cad_drilled_block.py`) binds any part described by faces, arcs, turned outsides and holes along the model axes, knowing no feature by name: every listed band binds under `<feature>_<key>`, and a band located `_from` a face or feature is checked against the model. It is for families of simple drilled, reamed and tapped parts that share one route; it generates no pictures (plans ship authored palette SVGs). Keep it name-free: a check belongs to a kind or a field, never to a feature name.
- Turned bush: `turned_bush` (`src/prechips/cad_turned_bush.py`, pictures in `bush_diagrams.py`) binds a short part turned on one axis (bush, spacer, ring, washer, collar, cup, plug). Every station is a distance from the `datum_end` face into the part, whichever model axis and direction the export uses, so plans and pictures never handle signs. A diameter is one of three acceptances: a `dia` band, a `stock` size left as bought (checked equal to its bar; a member's outer comes off its own `stock_dia_<member>_in` bar) or a `dia_ref` turned to a measured mate's `diametral_clearance`; never print the REF as a target. Pictures are side elevations from the operator's side, chuck left; `data-holes` names holes made in an earlier setup.
- Picture palette (#405): every hobby picture draws by role from `src/prechips/palette.py` (layouts append to role groups; templates use `class="<role>"`), never by colour; classes that name no role are left alone, so authored SVGs keep their own CSS classes, ids and markers (the palette's defs are `prechips-*`: `url(#prechips-arr)`, `cad_diagrams.ARROW`). `tests/test_palette.py` renders every example hobby traveler and refuses a picture colour (fill, stroke, style) that no role gives. Jaws, chuck jaws, soft jaws, shims, straps and toe clamps are `clamp`, keyed "vise jaw, work holder" (keep part names such as "strap" and "clamp" out of the key, and "hold-down", which names hardware; text on clamps `label-on-clamp`; a per-element dash needs a callout, since the key shows one look per role); 1-2-3 blocks, parallels, jacks, rests, step blocks and stop blocks `fixture-support`; tape or adhesive under a part `bond`; hold-down screws, washers and plugs hardware, `part-rest`; end mills, drills, taps, blades and edge finders `cutter`; cut sections `section`; parted waste `part-waste`; coordinate axes and viewing-direction arrows `axis` (not keyed). Each role differs in greyscale by lightness, pattern or line style. The traveler prints the palette's legend once, after the tool list, listing only the roles its pictures draw; pictures carry none. Feed arrows (#416) show the cutter's path over the part and are labelled "cutter path"; table motion is named only in setup text, because climb or conventional follows from which side of the cutter's path the material lies.
- Turning gate: both built-in profiles enforce `turning_deflection`, with authored total-transverse-bending limits of 0.10 mm rough and 0.005 mm finish. Exceedance or missing applicable data fails before output; success and explicit opt-outs add no checker text, warnings or certificates to the traveler. Keep stock/diagram-only fixtures independent with explicit opt-outs where needed.
- Require explicit setup `machine_kind` and lathe-operation `cut_kind` coverage. Only declared continuous solid-round longitudinal cuts are modeled; facing, parting, grooves and attachment domes are explicitly `other`, not silently checked. Do not infer material identities, geometry or numeric feed from prose. Use typed CAD/process bindings for analysis data without changing printed output.
- Keep coefficients editable in packaged `turning.toml`; partial custom TOML overrides merge over defaults. Profile `turning_config` paths are profile-relative; `--turning-config` wins. Repeatable `--disable-check` removes selected enforcement, including `turning_deflection`, without sheet warnings. Three checks read that one config: `turning_deflection`, `milling_deflection` and `drill_pecking` (each `CHECKS` entry with `config="turning"` is a reader). It is read only while at least one reader is enforced. Protect active default/override configuration inputs from output aliases. Per-cut `max_deflection_mm` may only tighten the configured phase limit.
- Default material conditions are rolled-hard aluminum (2014-T6 modulus proxy), medium brass (naval-brass modulus proxy), medium bronze (phosphor-bronze ASTM B159 modulus proxy), authored plain-carbon 160–180 HB for 1018 (SAE 1025 modulus proxy), and authored annealed tool-steel 200–250 HB for O1 (steel-family modulus proxy), never hardened O1. v0.11 adds `aluminum_6061` (6061-T6 modulus, rolled-hard aluminium unit power) and `stainless_304` (authored annealed proxy at most 200 HB: stainless 175–200 HB unit power, 18-8 modulus; not cold-worked or work-hardened stock), plus handbook drill thrust `drill_kd` for every material except bronze, which deliberately has none (Table 8 has no bronze row, so drilling bronze refuses until a shop config supplies one); `stainless_304` borrows the austenitic type 316 thrust row, which the handbook bases on limited tests. Keep the packaged handbook citations and proxy caveats; radial/tangential ratio 1.0 is authored, not a universal measurement.
- Preserve the shaft's actual feeds, DOCs, limits and support geometry. Limits apply to estimated radial bending at the tool (diameter error ≈ 2×); the tangential force is reported, not compared. Every cut declares the full traverse (`start_z_mm`/`end_z_mm`) and the retained extent (`kept_start_z_mm`/`kept_end_z_mm`, mandatory, never defaulted); the limit applies over the kept range, support validation covers the whole traverse, and refusals report both maxima. Bind kept bounds from CAD/process values (shaft: `north_apex_z`, `body_kept_end_z`). With them the journal passes and the body finish still refuses at Op 60 (about 0.009 mm radial just before follower engagement at Z152, inside the kept body). Do not tune data to manufacture a pass. Share authored longitudinal feed parameters between numeric analysis records and printed instructions/settings. Bound maximum DOC at final/minimum diameter without assuming a rough-pass order. This remains a static prototype screen, not measured diameter-error prediction, machine-compliance/chatter modeling or machining approval.
- v0.11 milling deflection (Pedro, 2026-11-13): both built-in profiles enforce `milling_deflection` (`src/prechips/milling.py`) on every mill and drill-press cut, vise and table holds alike. Every non-lathe setup declares `beam` (`cantilever` from a clamp span, `between` two pinned supports with overhangs, or `solid` over a stated seat; `"other"` only where no operation cuts with the spindle) and every spindle operation `milling_cuts` (`"other"` only when the spindle removes no material); an operation's own `beam` replaces the setup's table. Each cut declares `stations_mm` (points) or `traverse_mm` (a pass along the beam, judged at its worst point); a cut's `rpm` must equal its operation's. Loaded axes are derived, never declared: milling bends both axes across the beam, thrust only along z, and thrust along an upright (z) beam compresses it without reading thrust data; a solid seat takes only drill or plunge thrust. Forces are the handbook mean milling force (unit power × removal rate ÷ cutting speed, `turning.cutting_force_n`, shared with turning) and drill thrust; `[milling]`/`[drilling]` in `turning.toml` hold the authored ratios (radial and axial 1.0), wear factors and limits (0.10 mm rough, 0.025 mm finish: one-tenth of the .XXX band). Do not tune ratios, limits or declarations to pass: declare every load a step names (a plunge entry, a drill) and carry thrust on a stated support. Examples that do not declare beams and cuts yet render with `--disable-check milling_deflection`. Owner rulings 2026-11-16 (#407–#410, #429, #430): a tool's stated `axial_ratio` (any milling kind) or `helix_deg` (tan(helix), side and slot cuts) replaces the configured axial ratio, and the refusal names the source; author neither without a vendor or measured source. A rough cut may declare `finish_cut` (the next finish cut in its operation, uniquely named there) and `finish_stock_mm` (per loaded axis: that cut's stock, or `"through"`), compared instead of the rough limit; `max_deflection_mm` still applies on every axis, `"through"` included; nothing is inferred from the finish cut. Plans may set `milling_material` for cuts that omit `material` (kits keep it per cut). A shared setting's numeric `chip_load_mm_per_tooth` and `flutes` ride on the loaded operation and bind its cuts, analysed at the larger of the chip load and the printed (rounded) feed per tooth; feed text is never parsed. Every cantilever in a vise setup, setup-level or an operation's, declares `vise_left` (`start`/`end`: the grip measured from the hold's declared overhang end, the beam no longer than the hold's part; `other` when it is not the hold's part along the jaws); only such a link reads `vise_hold`, and only the linked hold. A rough cut's `finish_stock_mm` with every axis `"through"` needs `max_deflection_mm`. The CLI loads the turning/milling config once, passes the parsed dict to every enforced reader, and a config refusal names every enforced reader and its `--disable-check`.
- v0.11 plate hold: `beam.hold = "plate"` (`seat`, `seat_mm`, `clamps_mm`, `section_mm`) is a thin part on a fixture plate. A downward push bends nothing; lift (side and slot cuts) and side load bend the part between clamp stations, pinned between the adjacent pair and an overhang beyond the end pair. `tabs` (`stations_mm`, `section_mm`, `length_mm`) declares the cut-out stage: the tabs replace the clamps, give as short cantilevers with shear, share the force along the part, and refuse unless each sits at a clamp station (the margin strip's bending is not modelled). `bond` (`shear_mpa`, `tensile_mpa`, `derating`, `pieces`) declares a part stuck to the plate instead (exactly one of `clamps_mm` and `bond`; no `section_mm` or `tabs`): nothing bends, each cut's cross-spindle force is compared with derated shear × area under every piece, and a side or slot cut's lift with the derated tensile strength as 4 × force over a part's width-square patch or a waste piece's whole area. Screws and pins get no credit. A vise that holds the plate declares `vise_hold.grips = "plate"` with the plate's sizes, and a plate beam refuses a hold that grips the part. Plates and fences draw as the `fixture-plate` role, edge clamps as `clamp`. Keep the strap-like worked example's refusals in `tests/test_milling.py` (full-depth slot, cuts past the end clamps or tabs); do not tune stations, tabs or limits to pass. Not judged: clamp force and slip, tab strength, lift at the edge away from the clamps; for a bond, peel, dwell and contamination beyond the stated derating.
- v0.11 drill pecking (#403): both built-in profiles enforce `drill_pecking` (`src/prechips/pecking.py`). Every operation with a tool declares `drilling`: `"twist_drill"` (mill, drill press or lathe tailstock) or `"none"` (spots, countersinks, reamers, end mills, taps, saws, turning tools); the requirement never reads another check's data. A twist-drilled hole declares `peck` (`"none"` or `{ step_mm, retract = "chip_break" | "full" }`) and `hole_depth_mm`; Ø is the tool's `diameter_mm`. L/D bands (none ≤ 3, chip-break ≤ 5, full beyond, refuse > 10; edges with 1e-9 relative slack) and step ≤ 1 × Ø are cited rules of thumb in `turning.toml` `[pecking]`, one set for all materials (no source gives a numeric shift). Pecking at L/D ≤ 3 needs `peck_reason`. Bind each peck step or depth from one mm process parameter into both the field and the instruction (`{{process.x_peck_mm:in.3dot}}`; the `in` prefix refuses non-`_mm` names); do not type it twice.
- Pro prints only what an experienced operator needs: tools, setups, feeds, speeds and inspections, with the dimensions and instructions necessary to perform them. No tutorials, repeated warnings, checker reports or internal identifiers. Omit routine technique and generic safety reminders ("lock head", "short stickout", "power off", stopping/retracting, cleaning/reseating); retain part-specific workholding, datums and cutting requirements.
- For a requested hobby method, author concise, actionable, part-specific `instruction_detailed` in the operation itself. It replaces the pro instruction, never follows or repeats it; retain shared settings and inspection limits. Explain unfamiliar attachment setup once, then reference that method with each later operation's own dimensions.
- Steps (#454, Pedro 2026-11-17): an operation's compact slot is `instruction` or `steps`, its detailed slot `instruction_detailed` or `steps_detailed`, never both in one slot. Each step is `{ kind = "action" | "check" | "stop", text }`; the sheet labels check and stop lines (label, rule, weight: distinct in greyscale), so the text never repeats "Check:" or "Stop:". Use steps when an operation mixes routine work with checks or stop conditions; pro prints one line per step, no extra spacing. Instruction and step text take `**bold**`, `*em*` and `` `code` ``; plain instructions also take whole-line `1. ` numbered and `* ` bulleted lists. A `- ` line never makes a list and always prints as typed, so tolerances keep their minus signs (Main's final ruling, 2026-11-17); do not add heuristics for `- `. Text is escaped before markup, and strings without markup, and sheets without steps, must render byte-identical.
- Operation pictures (Pedro 2026-11-17): an operation may carry its own `diagram` (the setup's `{ file, alt }` shape), printed just before that operation's row, for a later stage of one clamping. A setup keeps at most one picture of its own; `setup_diagrams` prints both kinds, but its enforcement requires only the setup picture, which an operation picture never replaces. Sheets without operation pictures keep their exact bytes.
- Shop ownership and stock (Pedro, approved 2026-11-19): shop `[tools]`, `[gauges]`, `[workholding]` and `[stock]` records may carry `status` (`on_hand`, `not_received`, `to_order`, `to_make`; absent means not recorded), and plan tools may override a shared tool's status. `[stock.<key>]` holds `description`, `name` and `status`; plans name what they need with `stock_refs`, while `material`/`stock` text stays the printed authority. The tooling list and stock line mark items not on hand ("to order", "not received", "to make"); gauge and workholding status is never printed, and sheets without such marks keep their exact bytes. `shop` may name a directory whose `*.toml` files (dot-names skipped) merge by section, refusing a key defined in two files. `status` records only what the shop file says: prechips does not track quantities, orders or receipts and keeps no inventory database.
- Specify datum locations and acceptance dimensions, not generic measuring-tool technique. Pro omits edge-finder speeds, hand-feed instructions, tip-radius offsets and compensated inspection readings; hobby may explain a source-backed attachment measurement needed to carry out the job.
- The pro base-bar traveler must print on no more than two Letter pages at readable type size. Hobby may use extra pages for readable setup diagrams and requested specific methods. ## Keep it small
